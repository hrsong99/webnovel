# Chapter 6. The First Crack

A broken loom leg had been driven into the ground like a stake at the entrance to Mulle Valley.

During the three-day descent from Blackwind Mountain, Jin Cheol did nothing but match his steps to his breathing. By the second day, his Unraveling Qi lasted ten breaths without breaking. On the third, he could feel the slender current below his navel reach the soles of his feet without vomiting. Only then did Seo Muhan judge that he had reached the entry of the third-rate realm. More than half a month had passed since the torn flesh of his shoulder had been stitched, and a scab had formed, but Seo Muhan still forbade any movement that twisted his left ribs too sharply.

Above the broken loom leg flew the blue banner of the Unmun Escort Agency. It marked a village that paid protection fees. Beneath the banner sat twelve sacks of grain and a twelve-year-old boy named Gangho.

Yeonhwa ran to the child.

“Who made you sit here?”

“My mother.”

Gangho did not cry. As though reciting words he had memorized, he repeated that the Unmun Escort Agency would feed him and teach him martial arts. The crescents left by his mother’s fingernails were still vivid on the inside of his wrist, where she had tried not to let him go.

Jin Cheol reached for the banner. Yeonhwa caught his wrist.

“If you pull it down now, the fight begins today. Prepare first.”

“They’re taking the child today.”

“And when you charge in first and get beaten down again, who will stop them?”

Jin Cheol released the pole. Gaining the Sutra of Unraveling had not changed much. The Unraveling Qi gathered beneath his navel was hardly more than a thread. The first step of Drifting Step began by narrowing the distance between his right and left foot, but the wide stance worn into him by years at the forge kept returning.

Do Changsu arrived at sunset. He had brought two subordinates, but entered the village alone. At the sight of Jin Cheol, he pushed the hilt of his saber forward with his thumb.

“So you were with old Seo after all.”

“Leave the child.”

“The agreement says that if the protection fee falls short, we take payment in people.”

Yeonhwa stepped forward with the ledger. “Who made that agreement?”

“The former village head.”

“He died at Cheonggye Village. You mean to take a living child with a dead man’s seal?”

Do Changsu’s gaze stopped on the ledger. He made no effort to smile.

“I don’t enjoy this either. But if I fail to meet this month’s tribute, my sister and nephew are thrown out of the agency’s inner compound. If being thrown out is all that happens, they’ll be fortunate. I still owe the debt for my initiation fee.”

“Why should this village repay your debt?” Jin Cheol asked.

“Then who should? The debts of this world flow downward. I’m being crushed from above as well.”

Do Changsu tapped his belly.

“You sniffed around under a few sect gates. You should know. Did you think a scrap of martial arts came free? I was beaten and starved from the age of thirteen to open this dantian. If I retreat now, those years and my family vanish together.”

When he finished, Do Changsu tightened the knot on the escort agency’s banner twice. His hands had paused only when he spoke of his sister and nephew.

“Give us two days,” Yeonhwa said. “I’ll compare how much you actually collect with how much the Unmun Escort Agency receives.”

“And what will knowing that do?”

“Separate what the village owes from what you skimmed.”

Do Changsu’s eyes chilled. “Still sharp with figures after losing two fingers, I see.”

“It made me better. I can’t afford mistakes with what remains.”

Even as she answered, Do Changsu’s eyes never left the ledger. He granted them two days and took a room at the Mulle Valley inn. He posted a subordinate by the window, as if to watch every road used by anyone carrying a ledger. He knew that Jin Cheol followed from a distance, but merely snorted at a man who had only just entered the third-rate realm.

For those two days, Jin Cheol did not trail Do Changsu in plain sight. He had the people drawing water at the well, the owner of the forge’s grindstone, and the granary keeper record when Do Changsu appeared and which foot moved first. Then he compared their notes with his own observations.

Every morning, Do Changsu washed his saber at the well. At noon, he used the village forge’s grindstone. In the evening, he circulated qi inside an empty grain store. When he went to collect protection fees, he stepped first with his right foot. When someone refused, he pressed the scabbard with his left hand, then drew two short breaths. It was the same habit Jin Cheol had seen at Cheongu Hall.

With a palm against the rim of the well, Jin Cheol could feel Do Changsu’s footsteps travel through the stone. He held the Unraveling Qi beneath his navel and gathered his senses through the Mind’s Eye until his insides throbbed. Qi did not appear as light. But when Do Changsu circulated it, the fine ripples on the water changed. Water that ordinarily followed the wind drew inward toward the center the moment he held his breath.

Late third-rate. At that level, he could circulate internal energy through the major meridians and reinforce his body. A single head-on strike from his scabbard could break the bones of Jin Cheol, who stood only at the threshold of third-rate.

Seo Muhan nodded when Jin Cheol gave his assessment.

“Seeing is not the same as reaching. Knowing first does not make your body arrive first.”

“Then I reduce the distance my body has to travel.”

Jin Cheol did not imitate Do Changsu’s footwork. He marked the places the man always stepped. The narrow lane between the well and the forge had the wall of the weaving room on its left and the well on its right. There was no room for a broad saber stroke.

The first line of Drifting Step did not teach speed. It told the practitioner to move half a step aside from the place where an opponent’s strength became complete. Jin Cheol practiced that half-step through the night. He corrected the way the burns on his right arm made him lean left and pivoted on his heel without lifting his foot. Once in every twenty attempts, he managed the movement without pain in his ribs.

Yeonhwa made different preparations. She pasted the common ledger and the receipts issued by the Unmun Escort Agency onto a courtyard wall. Over the last two years, the village had paid seventy-eight seom of grain. The agency recorded receiving forty-one. Of the difference, twelve seom had gone toward interest on Do Changsu’s initiation fee. The rest had gone to Cheongu Hall and the escort guards between him and the agency.

Copies were sent to three destinations. Obok’s nephew carried one set, together with the testimony of the six rescued children, into the information network linking nine victim villages. A table comparing the receipts went to Clerk Bak Munsu at the magistrate’s office, the man who had handed them the secret ledger two years earlier. Bak Munsu replied that he would enter the date of receipt in the official log. The last set went aboard an herb merchant’s wagon to the contracts office of the Cheongha Escort Agency, which competed with Unmun for convoy work. Cheongha sent back a letter: if the forced tribute was confirmed, it would take over the contracts of three merchant companies traveling through Mulle Valley and post the reason in the market town.

Half-tallies from all three replies reached Mulle Valley. Even if Do Changsu tore up the ledger or cut down its keepers, the victims’ testimony, the magistrate’s date of receipt, and the cancellation of the escort contracts would each proceed on its own.

The villagers murmured. Those whose rice Do Changsu had taken stood beside those who had once asked his subordinates to drive away bandits. It was not simple to call him only an enemy.

“From today, we do not pay one household at a time,” Yeonhwa said. “Every payment goes into one ledger. Unless the Unmun Escort Agency signs for it, not one grain leaves this village.”

“What if they draw their sabers?” someone asked.

“Then we gather, so no one takes the beating alone.”

She counted the homes with hammers and those with loom shuttles. There were seventeen. She did not ask them to use the tools in a fight. She asked them to make noise at an appointed rhythm, masking Do Changsu’s breathing and disrupting the cadence Jin Cheol had memorized.

Before the villagers, Yeonhwa also read aloud the conditions imposed on the day they found the Sutra of Unraveling. She asked whether Jin Cheol could bring out the resonance implement in another person’s presence. The seventeen households, including Gangho’s mother, heard how it would be used and when its use must stop, then pressed their handprints to the page. Only then did Seo Muhan take the resonance implement from his medicine case and hand it to Jin Cheol.

At sunset on the second promised day, Do Changsu arrived with six subordinates. His jaw tightened the moment he saw the ledger on the wall.

“Take it down.”

One of his men approached. The villagers blocked his way. They carried sickles and hoes, but did not raise them first.

Yeonhwa lifted a copy of the ledger high.

“I sent it to nine victim villages, Clerk Bak Munsu at the magistrate’s office, and the contracts office of the Cheongha Escort Agency. Tear down what’s on this wall and nine villages release the children’s testimony, the magistrate records today’s date, and three merchant companies cancel their contracts with Unmun.”

One of the subordinates, Bak Seok, moved closer to the wall. Beneath the name of his father, Bak Nosik, he read the same line twice: *Funeral expenses: three nyang of silver. Interest after repayment of principal: seven nyang.*

“The agency paid only one nyang of silver for my father’s funeral. Where did the rest of this debt come from?”

Another subordinate, Yu Gap, found his sister Yu Wol’s name. Two nyang for medicine had become a nine-nyang initiation deposit. When Do Changsu ordered them to tear down the ledger, Yu Gap lowered his saber hand.

“Explain my sister’s debt first. I will not take down this paper.”

Bak Seok also stepped out of the road. The remaining four merely looked between the two men and the villagers. None drew a saber.

Do Changsu leveled his scabbard at Jin Cheol.

“You’ve learned to hide behind other people.”

“Today I don’t take the beating alone.”

Do Changsu kicked off the ground.

At that instant, hammers rang throughout the village.

Bang. Bang. Loom shuttles struck walls. Sounds that seemed regular, then slipped aside by one beat, filled the alley. Do Changsu’s first breath vanished beneath the noise.

Jin Cheol ignored the noise and watched Do Changsu’s left hand and right heel. Keeping Unraveling Qi inside his own belly and legs, he followed Do Changsu’s current with the Mind’s Eye. He had seen the motion more than twenty times: just before the qi reached the shoulder, Do Changsu’s left ribs opened.

Jin Cheol shifted half a step.

The scabbard passed before his nose and buried itself in the wall. Stone dust scored his cheek. Even forewarned, Jin Cheol failed to evade it entirely. It clipped his shoulder, and the limb went numb.

Do Changsu kicked off the wall and turned. He was faster than Jin Cheol’s body. The second blow struck the old bruises across his ribs. The sound of bone cracking came from inside him, and his Unraveling Qi scattered. Jin Cheol slammed back against the rim of the well.

“This is the martial art you stole?”

Do Changsu drew his saber. There was no blade aura along its edge, but the internal energy running through it raised a low vibration.

Swallowing blood, Jin Cheol brought out the resonance implement. It was a crude thing: three nails hanging from a single ring. It could not block a blade or fell a man. He pressed the ring against the well stone and gave it a short flick.

Of the three nails, the bent one rang first. Its vibration matched Do Changsu’s saber.

Jin Cheol used the sound as his marker. He gathered his scattered Unraveling Qi only within his belly and ankles, using it to keep himself upright. The hammers and shuttles burst louder. When Do Changsu abandoned his hearing and used internal energy to steady his center, the well water drew inward.

Two, one, pause.

Jin Cheol entered before the saber emerged. He was not faster. He had chosen the shortest path. He ducked beneath the blade and set his left foot outside Do Changsu’s right. For the first time, the opening step of Drifting Step did not break apart.

Do Changsu’s elbow fell upon Jin Cheol’s back. Pain tore through the burn scars, blackening his sight. Still he did not retreat. He drew the short chisel from inside his robe, set its point beneath the saber’s guard, and braced the butt against the rim of the well. A bridge of iron now linked saber, wrist, and well stone.

At the instant the bent nail trembled hardest, a heavy hammer in the alley struck the well’s foundation stone. The hammer’s vibration—not Unraveling Qi, which remained inside Jin Cheol—ran through the chisel, saber guard, and bones of Do Changsu’s wrist.

At the physical shock ringing through his wrist, Do Changsu reflexively opened his fingers, then clenched them again. That brief correction overlapped the final beat of the cadence he had memorized—*two, one, pause*. He sent internal energy into his arm half a beat too early. His wrist, not yet locked, received the force of his own body.

The saber fell into the well.

Do Changsu clutched his wrist. Neither meridians nor dantian had been injured. In a few breaths, the numbness would fade and his qi circulation would recover its rhythm.

Jin Cheol did not put the chisel to Do Changsu’s throat. Instead, he kicked away the scabbard that had fallen beside the well.

“Give us the tribute ledger.”

“You won’t kill me?”

“If you die, the debts die with you, along with the names of those above you.”

Do Changsu’s qi circulation began to return. Jin Cheol could hold on no longer. The moment he let the chisel fall, his knees buckled. He collapsed chest-first against the corner of the well, and the cracked rib finally broke. The seventeen villagers filled the space behind him. Bak Seok and Yu Gap stood not with Do Changsu, but before the ledger on the wall.

Do Changsu could have killed Jin Cheol bare-handed. But his gaze moved from Bak Munsu’s receipt to Cheongha’s tally, then to the debt entries bearing his sister’s and nephew’s names. His foot started toward the saber and stopped. He counted the cost—and retreated.

Do Changsu took a small book from inside his robe and threw it to the ground.

“Do not touch my sister or my nephew.”

Yeonhwa picked it up. “Their names will go in a different column from their debts.”

The Unmun Escort Agency’s banner came down. The villagers carried the sacks of grain back to the storehouse. Yeonhwa asked Gangho whether he wanted to go to his mother or first meet the six children living at Obok’s farm. Holding his mother’s hand, Gangho chose to meet the children. He said he would decide where he wished to learn after he had seen the place.

Jin Cheol had a broken rib, and the Unmun Escort Agency still stood. It was not enough to call a victory. Yet from that day on, Mulle Valley was no longer stripped one household at a time.

The villagers wanted to burn their agreement with Unmun. Yeonhwa stopped them. Even an unjust contract preserved whose seals had been placed upon it, she said, and that would be evidence in the next fight. The paper went into an iron chest in the common storehouse. Three different households each received one key. It was their first rule to ensure that no single person could own the village’s victory.

A new rule was affixed behind the old agreement. *No child shall be surrendered in payment of debt, protection fees, or initiation fees. A returned child shall choose their own home and learning.* Of the six children at the northern farm, two sent word that they would return to their families once those families’ debts had been erased from the ledger. The other four chose to remain with Obok for the time being. The villagers promised grain and witnesses to uphold both choices.

Do Changsu and his subordinates withdrew empty-handed. He looked back once before crossing the pass. His expression held less resentment than warning: a greater creditor was coming behind him.

That night, as Yeonhwa bandaged Jin Cheol’s wounds, she asked a question.

Though their wedding remained postponed, Yeonhwa wound the bandage with her right hand and used the three fingers remaining on her left to keep the knot from slipping. When Jin Cheol tried to lay his hand over hers, she put the medicine bowl into it instead.

“If you reach the end of the Sutra of Unraveling, can you truly erase another person’s dantian?”

“That is what it said.”

“Then you will still be the one choosing who is allowed to keep power.”

“Anyone who buys children with a saber should have it taken away.”

“If you could have erased Do Changsu’s dantian forever today, would you have done it?”

Jin Cheol almost answered at once, then stopped. Do Changsu’s power crushed other people. It was also the only livelihood keeping his family inside the agency compound. Erasing his dantian might reduce the number of victims—and create others.

“I don’t know.”

Yeonhwa pulled the knot tight. When Jin Cheol groaned, her hand eased a little.

“I’ll put that answer in the ledger. When you start saying you know too easily, I’ll show it to you.”

Hundreds of li from Mulle Valley, in the Murim Alliance’s records archive, Cheon Muryeong had three reports spread side by side that same night.

The first was an account of the disturbance in Cheongu Hall’s cellar, written by one of its instructors. It mentioned gray-bearded Seo Muhan, a burned blacksmith, and the brief clang of a hammer striking a scabbard. The second was a liaison office document investigating the fire at the dye works. Seo Muhan’s coroner’s tag and a five-ring mark had disappeared, and a neighbor had testified to hearing pieces of iron of different lengths struck in succession in the alley. The third was a copy of Do Changsu’s incident report to the Unmun Escort Agency concerning Mulle Valley. Only this document contained his statement about the impact through the well stone, the resonance of three nails, and the numbness in his wrist that made him miss the timing of his own qi circulation. When an internal dispute broke out within the agency, a copy had passed to the Murim Alliance according to custom.

Cheon Muryeong separately listed only what the three reports could confirm. Seo Muhan and the burned blacksmith appeared together in the first two documents, and every one of the three incidents involved the sound of metal. He marked the three nails, which appeared for the first time in the last report, as physical evidence requiring separate confirmation. The dates leading from Cheongu Hall to the dye works and then to Mulle Valley also aligned.

In the final column, instead of a conclusion, he wrote one sentence and labeled it a hypothesis.

*The external physical stimulus produced by the same individuals may be related to Do Changsu’s failure of qi circulation. The reports alone cannot confirm the involvement of Unraveling Qi or any energy projected outside the body.*

He tied a red thread to the place-name Mulle Valley and took up his investigator’s badge. Before dawn, the records archive’s most exacting inspector departed for the south.
