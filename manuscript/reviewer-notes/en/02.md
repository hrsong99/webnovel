# Chapter 2 Editorial Overview

Fourteen graves for fifteen people stand beside the smaller grave containing Yeonhwa’s two severed fingers. Jin Cheol cannot accept Namgung Tae’s gold as a price for the dead, while Yeonhwa counts thirty-two surviving mouths and distinguishes what money can do from what it cannot. The magistrate refuses jurisdiction; the Murim Alliance office places dead people and damaged houses together beneath “compensation sufficient.”

Yeonhwa converts the twenty gold taels into a communal survival fund restricted first to rice, medicine, and roofs. That night, her three-fingered hand can no longer work an ordinary loom. She refuses Jin Cheol’s offered hand and sketches a new loom whose treadle opens the warp while her right hand carries the shuttle. Her injury therefore produces an independent need and invention rather than functioning only as Jin Cheol’s guilt.

Their investigation also begins with failure. A kitchen knife that seemed thrown by the blast was dropped while its owner cut a child’s rope, and witnesses disagree about direction. Only after preserving those contradictions do the remaining pieces of bent metal reveal the amplified rebound. The masters did not intend mass death, but chose the valley because their weapons rang more powerfully there and never considered whose lives would bear the risk.

Clerk Bak Munsu’s hidden ledger expands one disaster into forty-three cases across twelve years. Yeonhwa accepts the dangerous copy and devises date-only future tips; Jin Cheol helps put the papers into the clerk’s sleeve. The last entry already plans to rewrite Cheonggye’s fifteen deaths as lightning, turning the couple from victims of one duel into custodians of evidence against a recurring system.
