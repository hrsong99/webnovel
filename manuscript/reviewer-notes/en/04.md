# Chapter 4 Editorial Overview

Twelve days after the escape, Jin Cheol and Seo Muhan hide in a morgue beneath an abandoned dye house. Blue resin embedded in the cut on their cart axle has already allowed Do Changsu’s men to trace the northern road; relocating the six children buys time but does not secure the refuge. The morgue places corpses, damaged weapons, and initiation-survivor records side by side, allowing the power system to emerge through inspectable evidence rather than doctrine alone.

Seo proposes that the dantian is not an organ but a wound in the soul, torn by trauma and made to draw in natural Qi through controlled breathing. Jin Cheol changes bowl positions in Seo’s charcoal-dust experiment and flags inconsistent temperatures and vessel sizes, testing even an explanation he wants to be true. Yeonhwa asks first whether the wound can be closed, exposing the decisive limitation: if accumulated Qi is not fully drained, forced closure drives it backward through meridians and organs and can kill the practitioner.

Seo’s confession that he killed a martial artist by draining only part of the stored Qi prevents depowering from becoming a simple instrument of justice. When Jin Cheol proposes helping volunteers first and then stripping power from abusers, Yeonhwa asks who may erase another person’s livelihood, status, and family support without consent. Although this is the volume’s most explanation-dense chapter, its sequence of experiment, failed case, ethical dispute, pursuit, and escape keeps each revelation attached to leverage and danger.

When Do arrives, the trio uses weapon noise, a tensioned hemp line, lime dust, and the corpse passage to escape. Jin Cheol leaves Seo’s unverified research notebook to burn, prioritizing people and the names of the dead, but retrieves a metal seal marked “Archive of Forsaken Obsessions, Blackwind Mountain” from the false-bottom drawer he noticed earlier. They lose the morgue and most of twenty years of research while remaining under pursuit, but the archive sought by people who wished to abandon martial power becomes a concrete next destination.
