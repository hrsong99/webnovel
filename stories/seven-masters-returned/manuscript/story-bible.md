# Story bible — 칠대고수가 죽고 점소이만 회귀했다

**Status:** premise canon locked; names, title, and first-volume outline still subject to the explicit open-question gate.

## One-line promise

Seven future grandmasters who lost the world send an ordinary innkeeper back fifteen years; as their argumentative spirits awaken inside him, he must save their younger selves from becoming the people who failed and unite seven incompatible factions before the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult conquers them all.

## Intended reader experience

The reader should repeatedly receive:

- the pleasure of Ryu knowing the important future before powerful people do;
- immediate overpowered reversals against early threats;
- another locked master awakening with a distinctive voice and martial core;
- comedy from legendary masters arguing in Ryu’s head and confronting their embarrassing younger selves;
- emotional contrast between who a master was and who they became;
- hidden opportunities recovered or redirected for greater effect;
- former rivals becoming useful, reluctant allies;
- visible construction of a coalition rather than abstract speeches about unity;
- future knowledge becoming less exact while Ryu’s own judgment becomes stronger;
- mundane inn labor producing original martial insight.

## Cosmology and return rules

1. There is one failed future and one return. The main story contains no repeating loop.
2. The return sends Ryu’s soul into his own body exactly fifteen years earlier.
3. The return creates a new branch. Changing history does not rewrite or erase the echoes’ fixed memories.
4. The seven masters consume their future lives to power the return. What survives in Ryu is a spiritual afterimage with personality, memory, emotion, regret, martial comprehension, and one martial core—not a second living body.
5. The seven younger masters exist normally in the new branch. They have no future memories and retain full moral autonomy.
6. An echo may advise Ryu and lend power through its core. It may not permanently overwrite its younger self.
7. Brief synchronization, if later admitted, requires informed consent and may not transfer an entire future personality by force.
8. Ryu cannot return again. Death is final.

## Why Ryu is chosen

- He belongs to no sect or bloodline faction.
- All seven trusted him during the final war.
- He sheltered them at Return Inn and mediated disputes without exploiting secrets.
- The seven could not agree which of themselves should control the past.
- His empty cultivation foundation can accept seven incompatible cores.
- He understands ordinary people, logistics, debt, food, shelter, and consequences neglected by legendary warriors.

The selection is thematic: the masters’ only unanimous decision is that none of them should receive unilateral control of history.

## The inner Return Inn

Ryu’s mental world resembles Return Inn at night.

- Seven locked guest rooms contain the dormant echoes.
- A shared common room accumulates awakened masters.
- The counter resembles Ma Yeongsun’s but begins empty.
- Memory objects, weather, wounds, or sounds may appear when an archive reacts.
- A door opens through resonance among a relevant present event, the younger master’s formative turning point, and Ryu’s capacity to stabilize another core.
- Once awake, a master remains awake and may interact with all previously awakened masters.
- Not every master comments in every scene. Full ensemble arguments are reserved for decisions that warrant them.

## Ryu Dan

- Opening age: twenty.
- Role: Return Inn worker or junior keeper under Ma Yeongsun; exact ownership title remains to be locked.
- Starting state: no sect, no established internal cultivation, practical strength from labor, observant, socially underestimated.
- Future relationship: the one neutral person all seven trusted at the end of the war.
- External desire: prevent conquest and keep Ma and Return Inn alive.
- Internal need: stop treating the future masters’ certainty as authority and choose among incompatible truths.
- Power: seven memory archives, seven martial cores, fast technical comprehension, and eventual synthesis.
- Limit: his body cannot initially express endgame force; future memories conflict, omit other viewpoints, and lose predictive value as history diverges.
- Prohibition: he may not solve coalition problems by mind control, compelled possession, or becoming an unquestioned emperor.
- Signature development: serving, carrying, balancing, cleaning, accounting, and mediating become principles in an eighth martial path.

## Ma Yeongsun

- Age at opening: to be locked in the cast ledger; retain sturdy middle-aged innkeeper identity.
- Role: owner and governing authority of Return Inn.
- Function: family, material reality, comedy, consent, and institutional discipline.
- She does not become impressed into passivity by Ryu’s return.
- She controls the inn’s money, access, prices, and rules.
- Return Inn becomes coalition infrastructure because Ma makes cooperation operational, not because Ryu gives speeches alone.
- She cannot initially hear the spirits; whether she later detects them remains open.

## The seven future/younger pairs

### 1. Baek Mugyeol — sword and leadership

- Future echo: strongest orthodox sword master; disciplined, honorable, controlling, burdened by failed command.
- Younger self: brilliant Azure Peak or equivalent disciple whose arrogance has not yet hardened into command isolation.
- Personal failure: confuses responsibility with controlling every decision.
- Coalition contribution: shared command doctrine and willingness to yield authority.
- Awakening role: first voice and first explanation of the failed future.

### 2. Tang Soryeong — poison and medicine

- Future echo: ruthless physician who hoarded knowledge after trust cost her family.
- Younger self: idealistic healer not yet convinced attachment is weakness.
- Personal failure: turns protection into secrecy and unilateral sacrifice.
- Coalition contribution: shared antidote, field medicine, and research network.

### 3. Gwi Sam — information and survival

- Future echo: funny, perceptive information master who survived by abandoning people until survival was empty.
- Younger self: frightened thief or low-ranked Beggars’ Union runner.
- Personal failure: treats information only as leverage.
- Coalition contribution: reliable cross-faction intelligence compact.

### 4. Maehwajin — movement and freedom

- Future echo: supreme movement artist who equated freedom with permanent escape.
- Younger self: gifted but constrained by family, sect, or obligation.
- Personal failure: refuses belonging before others can constrain her.
- Coalition contribution: courier, evacuation, and rapid-response network.

### 5. Jang Cheolwoo — external arts and endurance

- Future echo: indomitable body master who believes suffering proves worth.
- Younger self: physically gifted trainee being harmed by destructive cultivation culture.
- Personal failure: converts abuse into doctrine.
- Coalition contribution: scalable common-fighter training, protection, and logistics.

### 6. Yeom Gaha — demonic sealing arts

- Future echo: dangerous, perceptive heir who expects rejection and strikes first.
- Younger self: persecuted successor not yet committed to the role others fear.
- Personal failure: treats preemptive hostility as the only honest relationship.
- Coalition contribution: alliance with unorthodox and excluded factions.

### 7. Seol Muyeon — formations and return theory

- Future echo: systems thinker who believes perfect structures can replace trust.
- Younger self: formation prodigy near the machinery or scholarship that will expose the coming conquest.
- Personal failure: prioritizes optimization over consent.
- Coalition contribution: voluntary combined formations and shared signaling.
- Awakening role: last door; holds the most complete explanation of the return and may have withheld information.

## Power system

### Memory archives

- Memories surface through context; Ryu does not receive an uninterrupted encyclopedia dump.
- Each archive contains its owner’s viewpoint, not objective truth.
- Overlapping memories can expose causal links no master recognized alone.
- Major historical events remain useful longer than exact schedules.
- Saved lives and changed institutions accelerate divergence.

### Martial cores

- Seven dormant cores correspond to the seven traditions.
- An awakened core grants immediately useful foundations, perception, and rapid relearning.
- Ryu begins overpowered relative to local gangsters and ordinary disciples after the first awakening.
- His young body cannot initially output a future grandmaster’s internal force, bloodline technique, or destructive endurance.
- Relevant resources, training, relationships, and changed turning points stabilize each core.
- Combining cores is more dangerous and more powerful than using one tradition cleanly.
- The final eighth path must arise from Ryu’s life and coalition practice, not from a hidden eighth download.

## Future knowledge curve

- Years 0–3: major events and many local causes remain highly reliable.
- Years 4–8: saved people and shifted resources make details unreliable.
- Years 9–12: strategic patterns remain useful; tactical predictions often fail.
- Years 13–15: the original history is gone. Ryu relies on people, institutions, and his own skill.

No scene may claim Ryu rehearsed an event that occurred only after the return point unless a specific future witness actually experienced and archived it.

## Macro-antagonistic force

The Heavenly Demon Divine Cult conquers the martial world in the failed future.

It wins through more than individual strength:

- unified command;
- shared doctrine and logistics;
- coordinated intelligence;
- mixed-unit formations;
- clear succession;
- willingness to adopt useful talent across origin.

The defending world fails through secrecy, prestige, factional exclusion, incompatible command, hoarded medicine, and preparation for postwar betrayal.

The Cult need not be uniformly evil. Some territories may prefer its order to predatory local clans. Individual arc opponents may be sincere administrators, fanatics, commanders, opportunists, old-order elites, or people resisting Ryu’s coalition for legitimate reasons. There is no requirement for one mastermind to appear in every arc.

## Return Inn’s institutional progression

Return Inn grows from roadside business into:

1. safe neutral meeting place;
2. intelligence exchange;
3. emergency clinic;
4. mixed training ground;
5. diplomatic venue;
6. supply and evacuation node;
7. coalition headquarters.

The inn must continue functioning materially. Food, rooms, debt, repairs, staff, consent, and Ma’s pricing remain plot-active.

## End-state proof

Fifteen years later:

- seven changed living masters cooperate voluntarily;
- their organizations function together without constant direct orders from Ryu;
- shared medicine, intelligence, logistics, formations, and succession survive disagreement;
- the future echoes accept that their younger selves are not replacement bodies;
- Ryu’s eighth martial path coordinates differences rather than erasing them;
- victory cannot reduce to killing one Heavenly Demon before the invasion begins.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- No second reset.
- No exact omniscience about newly created events.
- No permanent possession presented as harmless.
- No seven-voice exposition dump in the opening.
- No younger master becoming loyal merely because Ryu reveals future knowledge.
- No faction alliance existing only in dialogue; it must change resources, access, command, or risk.
- No Divine Cult portrayed as strong solely because every member is evil or innately superior.
- No future echo automatically correct about its younger self.
- No Ryu victory that makes the ensemble and institutions unnecessary.
