# Chapter 5. Two Carry, One Opens

The eastern warehouse had three doors.

Oh Gak said the goods left through the west door. When Ryu Dan examined the ruins in the failed future, there had been a broad cart gate in the southern wall. Nam Sogun’s scouts had found a new side door cut into the northern alley.

All three could be right. All three could be traps.

“I’ll block the western and northern routes,” Nam said. “If a cart emerges, bind the wheels first. Do not touch the barrels. If it turns toward the houses, do not pursue the men. Clear the road instead.”

The Azure Peak disciples answered crisply. The order was clear, and its purpose was not to make arrests but to keep the barrels safe.

The younger Tang Soryeong tied white cloth around their wrists.

“If this cloth turns yellow, holding your breath will not save you. Retreat upwind at once. Anyone who feels dizzy puts down his sword and sits. If you try to endure it heroically and collapse, I will hit you first.”

The younger Baek Mugyeol studied the height of the warehouse walls and roof.

“I will handle the traps and armed men inside. I will not cut a barrel without Physician Tang’s permission.”

“Do not cut one even with permission.”

“What if we need to examine the contents?”

“A physician opens it.”

“Then why did I bring a sword?”

“Use it when people prove less cooperative than barrels.”

Inside Ryu’s mind, the future Baek and Tang sighed at the same time.

*Were we always this hostile to each other?*

They had spoken in unison. Both turned to glare at the other.

Ryu did not have time to laugh. No carts had emerged from the warehouse. The bell had rung once, but everything after the departure signal was far too quiet.

“They know we’re here.”

Oh Gak nodded, his face bloodless.

“Deputy Leader Jang never waits. He’ll have moved first.”

“You stay here.”

“I know the routes better than anyone.”

“You couldn’t walk yesterday.”

“My mouth walks just fine.”

Since learning that he owed Ma Yeongsun twelve taels, Oh Gak had been desperate to prove his usefulness. Ryu had no intention of bringing him into a fight, but there was no need to waste the useful mouth.

“Stay beside Hero Nam and interpret the bell signals. Do not pick up a weapon.”

Oh Gak made an approximation of a martial salute.

“I obey the command of Hero Inn Boy.”

“I’ll deduct one tael from your debt each time you call me that.”

“Then I take it back.”

Hiding a smile, Nam led him away.

Ryu, the younger Baek, and the younger Tang approached the southern cart gate. Its handle was unlocked. The doors were thicker than Ryu remembered from the failed future, and the grooves in the ground were new.

Future Baek said, *There is a Falling Sword Formation over the door. Break the wire and blades descend from the ceiling.*

Ryu looked up. A thin wire ran between the rafters, but the dust lay unevenly across it.

“The position has changed.”

*The basic structure is the same. Remove the third nail in the right pillar.*

The younger Baek raised one hand first.

“Wait. The third nail is false.”

He pointed to the grain near the foot of the pillar. “They reinforced this post recently. The load is on the fifth nail.”

His future self examined the pillar in silence.

Ryu pressed the nail the younger Baek had indicated with the edge of his tray. Metal scraped above the door, but no blades fell.

The younger Baek looked at him.

“Who told you the third?”

“A guest with an old memory.”

“He was wrong.”

Future Baek’s expression became murderous.

*I was not wrong. The trap changed.*

Tang’s echo laughed.

*That is what being wrong means.*

The door opened.

There were no poison barrels inside.

Instead, twenty laborers knelt on the floor with their hands bound. Some had faces scarcely older than children. Others bore Black Tooth tattoos. Deputy Leader Jang stood behind them holding a torch. A scar cut across his left cheek and pulled his mouth into a permanent crooked smile.

“Come any closer and I light it.”

A shallow layer of oil covered the floor. There was no way to know whether Green Vein Poison had been mixed into it, and no time to test.

The younger Baek drew his sword halfway.

“Drop the torch.”

“Look at Young Master Baek, noticing people before barrels.” Deputy Leader Jang sneered. “You thought we started moving the goods yesterday? Half of it reached the market while your people were still arguing.”

The younger Tang’s face tightened.

Ryu looked beyond Jang to the rear wall. A sloping passage, absent in the failed future, descended underground. Three blue-banded barrels were disappearing down it. Ropes and pulleys moved without any laborer pulling them.

“The workers aren’t moving those.”

Jang’s smile vanished.

A low vibration rose through the floor. Symbols carved into the walls detected the energies of three different martial traditions: sword qi derived from Azure Peak, the Black Tooth Gang’s coarse internal force, and an unknown current of cold energy. Together, they powered a single pulley.

Future Baek said, *A composite formation. This is not the Falling Sword Formation I remember.*

Tang’s echo answered tersely. *The motion mixes the contents as the poison barrels descend. If one breaks, everyone inside is poisoned.*

No one knew the correct answer.

Ryu studied the present. The torch was in Deputy Leader Jang’s right hand. His left foot stood on a dry stone clear of the oil. Seven paces separated him from the younger Baek. The younger Tang needed twelve paces to reach the laborers. The underground pulley caught once every three turns.

“Young Hero Baek. Take the torch only. Do not try to capture Deputy Leader Jang.”

“You want me to let him escape?”

“Physician Tang. The instant the torch goes out, move the laborers south, into the wind. Do not cut the ropes before checking their bodies.”

“And you?”

“I’ll right the barrels.”

Both stared at him as if he had gone mad.

Deputy Leader Jang tipped the torch.

The younger Baek moved. Sword light shot forward in a straight line. Jang anticipated the strike and dragged one laborer up as a shield. But Baek’s sword sought neither man nor arm. The sword wind sliced the flame from the torch.

Darkness swallowed it.

At the same instant, six silver needles flew from the younger Tang’s hand and severed the knots binding the laborers. She covered the youngest worker’s nose and mouth with cloth and pushed him toward the southern door.

Ryu ran down the underground passage.

Behind him, Deputy Leader Jang cursed and fled. The younger Baek’s foot shifted once in pursuit, then stopped. The torch had been his responsibility. Instead of chasing, he stamped out the remaining embers.

At the bottom of the slope, one of the blue-banded barrels tilted. The iron hoop around it had caught in the formation’s groove. Forcing it loose would break the cask. Ryu caught it with both hands. The contents surged to one side.

*Sever the left groove with sword qi,* Baek said.

*No,* Tang snapped. *Any impact will make the poison surge inside.*

Two futures collided.

Ryu trusted his own body—the body that had spent twelve years rolling great wine casks, carrying soup cauldrons down stairs, and saving tables overturned by drunk customers.

He opened Baek’s and Tang’s cores together.

The first shared heartbeat.

The sword’s structural understanding revealed the line of force between the barrel and the groove. A physician’s perception showed where the liquid was gathering. The two forces pulled Ryu’s meridians in different directions.

He did not lift the barrel. He stepped half a pace to the right, following the tilt. Taking the weight through his waist, he wedged the tray beneath the cask and shifted the point of contact.

The second heartbeat.

The formation detected the two foreign energies. Symbols on the wall glowed red. If one tradition tried to drive out the other, the trap would explode.

Ryu did not push.

He guided Baek’s keen energy into the iron hoop caught in the groove and matched Tang’s fine current to the liquid’s counter-rotation inside the barrel. He did not blend them. He kept the currents separate, guiding each into a path the other could use next.

The barrel found level.

The third heartbeat.

Pain tore through Ryu’s chest. The first and second shared heartbeats had been safe. This third was the maximum overdraw his body could survive with two cores open.

He passed the barrel’s weight into his left foot. Another step. It was the same motion he used to rescue a tilting tray, except that twenty lives now rested on it.

“Returning dish.”

The words escaped him without conscious thought. A tray that came back. A step that did not erase the force it received, but returned it through the next foot.

The barrel slipped free of the iron groove.

The composite formation had not been attacked. Instead, when the two incoming energies traveled through it along separate paths, the pulley reversed. The other two barrels, which had been descending, slowly began to rise.

The floor of the passage split open. Within the crack flashed the same network of seven stars that lay beneath Return Inn.

Ryu hugged the barrel to his chest and rolled aside. Tearing pain spread from his right arm into his chest. A meridian had ruptured somewhere. The taste of blood filled his mouth.

A stone door emerged beneath the broken floor. Seven stars and an empty inn counter had been carved into its surface.

No one touched it, yet the formation vein that ran toward distant Return Inn gave a single pulse. The stone door opened one inch. Whatever kept watch beyond it did not appear.

The sense of something studying Ryu came not from beneath the warehouse, but from the opposite end of the formation vein—from under Return Inn itself. Had he tried to dominate, or had he connected? The wordless question returned to him.

Then the stone door stopped.

Ryu had no strength to investigate. He checked first whether the barrel had broken.

“Are you alive?” The younger Tang ran down the slope.

“Do you mean the barrel, or me?”

“If the barrel could answer, I would throw you away and treat it first.”

She seized his wrist. Her face hardened immediately.

“You used two different currents of internal energy at the same time. There are small tears in your meridians.”

Future Tang was furious.

*I said three heartbeats. I did not say to use the third until you were half dead.*

Future Baek spoke in a low voice.

*I should have ordered the pursuit.*

“You didn’t,” Ryu answered between breaths. “Not this time.”

The younger Baek came down with the laborers instead of Deputy Leader Jang. Jang was gone, but every worker was alive.

Nam had secured the northern route as well. He stopped two fleeing carts by binding only their wheels. Oh Gak rang the bell signal in reverse and drew the lower-ranking Black Tooth members into an empty alley. Most of those captured were hired laborers paid to move cargo. Only three knew what the poison was.

“Deputy Leader Jang?” Ryu asked.

The younger Baek wiped his blade.

“He escaped.”

“Do you regret it?”

“I do. But if I had chased him, the embers from his torch would still have been burning.”

Future Baek heard him.

*I do not remember making that choice.*

“It only exists in this branch.”

They found the thrice-folded ledger in the roof beam, exactly where Oh Gak had said. But someone had cut away the page naming the buyers. The surviving pages repeated the same standardization mark used on western mountain roads: a scale, a sack of grain, and a sword fitted inside one set of measured lines.

The younger Tang found the same mark on the bottom of the blue-banded barrels.

“They standardized more than the poison. The barrels and carts were made to the same measure.”

“A neighborhood gang like Black Tooth could not do this,” Nam said.

One torn sheet remained pasted to the wall. A strange phrase survived on it.

One Heaven, One Measure.

It sounded like order. It also sounded like a command.

Ryu remembered the Heavenly Demon Divine Cult of the failed future. It had not conquered the martial world through strength alone. While other sects argued over who owned a poison, the Divine Cult standardized the barrels, roads, and signals. It taught martial arts to talented commoners, and in return bound every art beneath a single command.

Both the virtue and the coercion of that system had already entered this warehouse.

Nam unfolded the field inventory slate.

“I will transport the seized goods and prisoners to Azure Peak.”

The younger Tang answered at once.

“The Tang Family takes the poison barrels and the poisoned.”

Just as the same fight threatened to begin again, the younger Baek placed the inventory slate between them.

“Senior Brother Nam will control routes and prisoner escort. Physician Tang will decide the handling of the poison barrels and patients. Both will sign the inventory, and the original will remain at Return Inn.”

Without looking at Ryu, he added, “Until the three-day agreement ends.”

The younger Tang studied him, then picked up the brush.

“Add that no one may open a poison barrel without medical authorization.”

“And that the Tang Family may not remove any portion of the evidence.”

“Must you write down something so obvious?”

“The obvious is always the first thing to disappear when no one writes it down.”

Future Baek spoke softly.

*I never said that.*

Tang’s echo answered, *Then listen carefully.*

Urgent bells sounded outside the warehouse. One, two, then three short strikes.

Oh Gak’s face changed.

“A false withdrawal signal. Deputy Leader Jang is calling in the men he has left.”

A resident came running from the northern alley.

“People are collapsing at the oil shops! Someone poured water into an oil cask and smoke—”

The younger Tang looked at the seized barrels.

“Some of the oil already reached the market.”

Nam opened the map. The younger Baek lifted his sword. Everyone looked at Ryu.

He tried to stand and fell to one knee. It felt as if his chest were splitting open. Both cores were spent. He could not open either again before a full night’s rest.

He planted a bloody hand on the inventory slate.

“Do not look at me.”

Uncertainty passed through their faces.

“Do the work you have already accepted. Hero Nam takes the roads. Physician Tang takes treatment. Young Hero Baek stops the false bells. I will return to Return Inn and coordinate the signals.”

“In that condition?” the younger Tang snapped.

“I won’t run.”

“You are forbidden to walk, too.”

At that moment, Ma Yeongsun’s freight cart appeared in the distance, carrying empty water barrels and cloth from the inn. Ma held the reins. One look at Ryu narrowed her eyes.

“Ask about the tray first.”

Ryu cradled the dented tray and smiled.

“Everyone survived.”

“So you stopped counting your own body as a person?”

She shoved him into the cart and snatched the inventory from his hand.

“If you intend to handle the market from my inn, do not use it as if it were your command post. I decide the keys, the ledgers, and the charges.”

Ryu nodded.

“That is why I’m going back.”

For the first time, Return Inn was about to stand in the middle of a war. But not one of its doors would open without its owner’s permission.
