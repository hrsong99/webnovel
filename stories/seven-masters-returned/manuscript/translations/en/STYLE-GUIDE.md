# English edition style and terminology guide

The Korean chapters in `manuscript/chapters/` are canonical. The English edition must read as original English-language wuxia/progression fantasy while preserving every event, limitation, custody rule, relationship, and deliberate uncertainty.

## Intended effect

Fast, concrete competence fantasy with dry domestic comedy, accumulating internal ensemble chemistry, and sincere emotional contrast between future masters and their younger living selves. Coalition-building must feel materially satisfying rather than bureaucratic.

## Narrative voice

- Close third person through Ryu Dan unless a locked scene explicitly requires otherwise.
- Practical attention: weight, heat, bowls, ledgers, doors, routes, breath, hands, and who owns the key.
- Contemporary-readable English without modern internet slang or faux-archaic diction.
- Rebuild Korean dry humor through timing, status, and attitude rather than literal syntax.
- Connect cause and qualification naturally; do not imitate Korean omitted subjects or clipped sentence order.
- Short sentences land a decision, joke, image, or blow. They are not the default rhythm.
- Keep the future echoes distinct through goals and reasoning, not catchphrases.
- Never describe Ryu as a passive vessel. He selects, rejects, combines, and pays.

## Return and identity terms

- 회귀 — return or regression according to sentence; avoid game-like “reset” unless a character is rejecting that concept
- 실패한 미래 — failed future
- 새 갈래 / 분기 — new branch / branch timeline
- 영혼 잔향 — future echo in ordinary narration; spiritual afterimage in formal explanation
- 내면의 귀래객잔 — inner Return Inn
- 객방 — guest room
- 무학핵 — martial core

The echo and younger living self are two valid continuations from shared history. Do not call the echo the “real” version or the younger self an “incomplete copy.”

## Martial and medical terms

- 내공 — internal energy
- 경맥 — meridians
- 운기 — circulate qi / qi circulation according to grammar
- 초식 — form or technique
- 보법 — footwork
- 검식 — sword form
- 독공 — poison arts
- 녹맥독 — Green Vein Poison
- 회반보 — Returning Dish Step
- 일곱 심박 — seven-heartbeat channel / seven heartbeats depending on context

Avoid numerical stat language. The heartbeats are bodily time and risk, not cooldown UI.

## Hierarchy and address

Translate titles by relationship and scene function. Use “Master” only for genuine teacher relationships or recognized grandmaster status. Younger Baek and future Baek must remain easy to distinguish through context without repeatedly labeling them “young” and “old” in every paragraph.

## Character voice anchors

- Ryu: quiet, exact, practical; jokes by treating grandeur as work.
- Ma: dry ownership language; asks who pays, who cleans, and who has a key.
- Future Baek: thirty-seven; formal, compressed, command-first, self-critical only when cornered.
- Future Tang: thirty-six; incisive, diagnostic, suspicious; affection appears as medical aggression.
- Living Baek: proud and adaptive, not stupid.
- Living Tang: compassionate and proprietary; can refuse both Ryu and her future echo.
- Nam: procedural, diligent, willing to revise based on evidence.
- Oh Gak: defensive humor and street survival; gratitude does not erase fear.

## Formatting

Each chapter begins exactly:

`# Chapter N. <Title>`

Then publishable prose only. Preserve scene breaks as `***`. Do not include translator notes, glossaries, summaries, or commentary inside chapter files.

## Volume One continuity constraints

- One return travels exactly fifteen years; there are no repeated loops or Day 31.
- Ryu begins at twenty with no internal energy.
- Future Baek wakes in Chapter 1; future Tang wakes in Chapter 2; both remain active.
- Ryu may channel one unstable core for up to seven heartbeats after a full night; only four are safe. With two cores, the first two shared heartbeats are safe and the third is a maximum-survivable overdraw that tears his meridians.
- Ma is forty-three, owns Return Inn outright, cannot hear echoes, and controls keys and custody.
- Oh Gak survives Chapter 2; his survival invalidates the failed future’s later schedule.
- Oh Gak leads the Chapter 1 collection party, returns to the warehouse after Ma expels him, and comes back poisoned with Azure Peak’s token in Chapter 2.
- Ryu returns Azure Peak’s token under receipt in Chapter 3.
- Living Tang releases only a bounded emergency protocol, not her family’s entire formula.
- Returning Dish Step is Ryu’s original movement, created in Chapter 5.
- Ryu’s meridians remain injured in Chapter 6 and both daily channels were spent in Chapter 5; he uses no borrowed channel in the finale.
- The Common Antidote Ledger ends under Return Inn custody with Azure Peak and Tang seals.
- Gwi Sam’s closed door leaks one pre-awakening sentence at the end; he cannot converse, advise, or lend power in Volume One.
- Myogak is not a central villain and remains unseen.

## Localization proof

After drafting English:

1. read all six chapters continuously for native voice;
2. compare numbers, objects, injuries, knowledge sources, custody, and awakened-core state to Korean;
3. verify all jokes still reveal status or relationship;
4. ensure institutional scenes remain dramatic through decisions and costs;
5. fix Korean first if translation exposes a source contradiction.
