# Chapter 3 Editorial Overview

After seventeen rejections in two years, Jin Cheol enters Cheongu Hall not as a disciple but as a traveling blacksmith. Do Changsu pays promptly and remembers subordinates’ names while emptying the grain jars of households behind on protection money. Yeonhwa, meanwhile, runs the survivor network and her treadle loom; their engagement remains suspended until they can renegotiate how they will live together and restrain one another.

Yeonhwa’s records and an almost unworn straw shoe point toward children who disappear after their families pay initiation fees. When seven children enter the hall, Jin Cheol notices a newly filed drain bar, wet-mugwort smoke, and a waiting herb cart—evidence that someone else has prepared an escape. Yet once he hears the children underwater, he cannot keep investigation separate from rescue and intervenes at once.

Do defends the rite through his own history: he survived burial alive, and the rice paid afterward carried his family through winter. Jin Cheol recognizes that parts of the justification may be true without accepting its conclusion. During the beating, he learns the heel-breath-scabbard cadence—two, one, pause—but his ordinary body is still too slow to exploit what he understands.

Seo Muhan’s smoke and cart combine with Jin Cheol’s hammer to finish the prepared exit. Six of the seven children escape; Hajin dies. Seo refuses to return the survivors automatically to families whose debts may endanger them, promising to ask what the children want first. At the close, he identifies Jin Cheol’s burn scar as the trace of a dantian that nearly opened—and his anomalous hearing as the faculty that opened instead.
