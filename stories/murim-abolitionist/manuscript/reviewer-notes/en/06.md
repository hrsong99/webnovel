# Chapter 6 Editorial Overview

At Mulle Valley, the Blue Cloud flag hangs above a broken loom leg and twelve sacks of grain beside Gangho, a separate eighth endangered child. Jin Cheol reaches for the flag; Yeonhwa stops him from becoming the first person beaten unconscious again. Do Changsu reveals that failure to meet his quota will endanger his sister and nephew, and Yeonhwa buys two days to compare the books.

The preparation does not unfold perfectly. Nine victim villages respond, but Bak Munsu does not, and Cheongha Escort Agency refuses to move without an official filing. What remains is the discrepancy visible to the villagers: seventy-eight seom collected, forty-one received by the escort agency. Seventeen households agree not to face retaliation alone and consent to the resonance tool’s limited use.

Bak Seok and Yu Gap find fabricated debts attached to their relatives. Do offers to forgive those debts, but cannot make “I will erase what never existed” sound like mercy. In the fight, Unraveling Qi remains inside Jin Cheol, supporting perception and posture only. The external force is ordinary physical vibration carried through hammers, nails, a chisel, and the well stone. Its timing overlaps Do’s reflexive correction and knocks the sword from his hand.

The result becomes rules and obligations rather than a clean triumph. Gangho and the six rescued children choose where to live and learn. Remembering Hajin, the villagers decide with real grain whether to protect Do’s sister and nephew while keeping his debts public. Asked whether he would have destroyed Do’s dantian forever, Jin Cheol answers, “I don’t know.” Elsewhere, Cheon Muryeong links the reports but explicitly refuses to claim that Unraveling Qi or projected power has been proven before leaving for Mulle Valley.
