# Chapter 1 Editorial Overview

The chapter opens on an ordinary afternoon in Cheonggye Village, grounding Jin Cheol’s unusual sensitivity to color, vibration, and stress in his work as a blacksmith rather than in martial talent. When Sword Saint Wi Sohyeon and Saber King Namgung Tae begin their friendly duel above the valley, Jin Cheol realizes that the entire mountain is accumulating their impacts through resonance. The villagers, captivated by the spectacle and the authority of the two masters, are slow to accept his warning.

Jin Cheol uses a plowshare and the village bell to drive an evacuation while Han Yeonhwa independently clears the streamside homes. Their choices save several residents and children, but a deflected force wave, amplified by the valley, breaks the cliff and erases the smithy. The quenching tank shields Jin Cheol from the pressure blast but leaves his right arm and back burned; Yeonhwa loses the ring and little fingers of her left hand under the loom, and Jin Cheol’s parents are among the dead.

Structurally, the chapter makes the social permission surrounding overwhelming power more important than the spectacle itself. Neither master intended the deaths, and Wi even shows sincere sorrow, but Namgung treats gold as a complete answer while Wi absorbs the victims into a claim about future lives saved by martial enlightenment. Jin Cheol’s decision to keep the gold as evidence, rather than reject it as tainted, begins the story’s shift from personal vengeance toward proof and institutional responsibility.

The masters leave without restraint, and Jin Cheol vows to use their money to melt their swords. At this point he has no internal energy or supernatural means of retaliation. His only leverage is his material knowledge, his demonstrated ability to read structural danger, and the painful principle that living victims must be helped before the dead can be mourned.
