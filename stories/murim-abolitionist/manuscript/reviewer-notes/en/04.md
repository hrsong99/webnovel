# Chapter 4 Editorial Overview

In Seo Muhan’s underground morgue, Jin Cheol reads the falsified account in a shattered sword before he studies the corpse. Seo tests his hypothesis—that inner power draws natural qi through a wound in the soul—with charcoal floating on water. Jin Cheol finds that a tilted table created the first apparent success. Only after they correct the setup does a narrower trace remain, establishing him as an investigator willing to challenge answers he needs to be true.

Yeonhwa records the rescue as “six of seven alive; Hajin dead,” keeping the child’s name beside the survivor count. Her first question is whether a wound can be closed. Seo’s answer makes abolition dangerous: seal a mature dantian without first releasing its accumulated qi and the trapped force destroys the practitioner from within.

Sedative powder on the dead man’s sword reveals that Seo killed a willing subject during an attempted severance. He admits that, after a sect killed his own son, he hid his failure to protest behind years of research on other people’s dead. Jin Cheol proposes ending murim through severance; Yeonhwa asks who gets to decide, and what happens to families whose food depends on the power being removed.

Do Changsu’s arrival converts the argument into immediate choices. Using metal resonance, loom tension, lime, and a corpse passage, the three escape while dividing the names and failure records among their clothes. Jin Cheol abandons the unverified research notebook but retrieves the five-ring seal. After recovery and distribution of surviving copies, the chapter ends on a decision rather than convalescence: they will go to Blackwind Mountain.
