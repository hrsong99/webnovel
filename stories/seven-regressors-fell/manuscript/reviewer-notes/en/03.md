# Chapter 3 Reviewer Overview

Chapter 3 puts Ryu Dan’s weakness and abnormal mastery on the same public scoreboard. The strength stone rates him only lower-grade Class B, preserving the body limits of his three opened nodes, but he then calculates wind, axle timing, and both faces of a broken wedge to ring all seven moving bells with one throw. The scene converts the story’s core rule—little power does not mean little understanding—into an immediate, legible payoff.

Nam Sogun is a capable rival who reasonably questions Ryu Dan’s possession of Baek Mugyeol’s Azure Peak Token without treating an inn servant as guilty by birth. He changes the seventh bell’s cord to defeat memorized solutions, then personally tests Ryu Dan with the Thirteen Falling Clouds. Ryu Dan calls and survives all thirteen forms, including Nam Sogun’s reordered ninth-form thrust, while mounting fatigue and a torn palm keep the feat physically costly.

The victory comes from exploiting the hitch after the Twelfth Form: Nam Sogun’s right big toe delays his hip and lifts his shoulder. Ryu Dan touches the examiner’s throat first with the broken wood, then offers correction rather than humiliation. When Nam Sogun immediately applies the advice and splits the training dummy, public verification upgrades Ryu Dan from a lucky servant to someone capable of correcting Azure Peak’s supreme art, earning him the materially consequential rank of provisional first.

The final reveal reclassifies the message—“On the thirty-first day, go to the Azure Peak Sect”—as a record engraved by the Seven-Star Return Formation through Ryu Dan’s own memory, not handwriting produced by a person. The pre-dawn outer-tomb survey therefore becomes more than an entrance requirement: the formation that completed 9,431 returns has deliberately summoned its surviving recording vessel. The chapter closes its status reward before opening the larger mystery of the formation’s intent.
