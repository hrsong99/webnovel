# Chapter 4 Reviewer Overview

Chapter 4 opens with Ryu Dan’s promise to bring all twelve members of the outer-tomb expedition back alive, then pays it down through three preempted traps: the poisoned pressure plate, the false life door, and the counterfeit Azure Peak Token. Nam Sogun records the rescues publicly, and Jin Hu and the other disciples begin obeying Ryu Dan in practice. Remembered knowledge is converted from private survival into official merit and real leadership.

Ryu Dan intercepts Gwak Hyeon before the betrayal that occurred in 317 loops, but the expected Gold-Masked patron is already dead and a seven-eyed monk anticipated Ryu Dan’s preemption. Blood replaces incense as the alternate signal, sealing the known exit and opening a blade corridor absent from all 9,431 repetitions. The chapter makes the post-divergence rule concrete: Ryu Dan’s successful interventions are now destroying the reliability of his remembered answers.

To cross the unknown corridor, Ryu Dan synthesizes his first original art, the Returning Dish Step, from the anchoring weight of the Plum-Shadow shoes, the circulation principle of the Myriad-Poison Ward detoxification method, and non-martial balance learned carrying trays. He turns invisible pressure into the force for each next step, then distributes the vault’s treasures according to each disciple’s needs instead of hoarding them. Their immediate use of those tools turns generosity into practical loyalty and increased party capability.

At the rear of the vault, Ryu Dan uses Yeom Gaha’s sealing breath to accept only the demonic energy his body can endure and opens his fourth star node, gaining control rather than an undefined power increase. The reward is counterweighted by the discovery that Seol Muyeon’s Outer Gate Key—the seventh foundation—is missing, with no bearer recorded by the formation. After fully delivering an original technique, allies, and node four, the ending supplies a precise new objective: find the unknown being who walked beyond remembered history first.
