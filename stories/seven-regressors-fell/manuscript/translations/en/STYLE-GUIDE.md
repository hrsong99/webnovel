# English edition style and terminology guide

The Korean chapters are canonical. The English edition should read as original English-language progression wuxia, not as a sentence-bound gloss.

## Intended effect

- Fast, confident, gratifying, and funny without becoming weightless.
- The reader should understand the near-term reward early and receive it cleanly.
- Preserve compact Korean timing where it lands prediction, humiliation, acquisition, or a decisive image; connect syntax where English needs causal flow.
- Avoid faux archaism, excessive cultivation jargon, defensive explanation, and generic crowd gasps.
- Ryu Dan’s competence is quiet and exact. Ma Yeongsun’s comedy is practical and unimpressed.

## Canon constraints

- The loop repeated 9,431 times and lasted at most thirty days.
- Chapter 1 begins on the first-ever day thirty-one.
- Ryu Dan is not a regressor; he was the formation’s recording vessel.
- His body reset during the loops. Knowledge is not physical capacity until star nodes open.
- The seven regressors died before Chapter 1.
- Ma Yeongsun remembers none of the loops.
- Ryu Dan opens node one in Chapter 1, nodes two and three in Chapter 2, and node four in Chapter 4.
- From Chapter 4 onward, deviations increasingly invalidate remembered events.
- Myogak can replay and recombine recorded openings into unseen sequences, but cannot originate or understand a genuinely new choice.
- The formation is dismantled in Chapter 6; retained skill and Ryu Dan’s original technique remain.

## Dialogue

- Status should be expressed through tactics, address, interruption, and who must explain themselves—not stiff pseudo-medieval English.
- Keep Ryu Dan’s predicted lines short enough to land as reversals.
- Ma Yeongsun does not speak like a modern meme account. Her humor comes from money, chores, food, and refusing martial grandeur.
