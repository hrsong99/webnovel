# Chapter 2. The Opportunity Had Seven Owners

Before the sun had fully set on the thirty-first day, three factions in Qingliu County received the same treasure map.

A note pinned with a black arrow flew into the Black Tooth Gang.

At the Green Snake Apothecary, run by poison merchants, half a map appeared beneath a crate of herbs.

A beggar sold one corner to the Iron Fist School for a bottle of wine.

Each fragment pointed to an abandoned mountain shrine north of the county seat. According to the writing, a “thousand-year elixir hidden by seven heroes” waited there.

There was no elixir, of course.

Ryu Dan had drawn the maps.

More precisely, he had copied one of the false maps the seven regressors had revised perhaps nine thousand times while deceiving one another. Baek Mugyeol had sent rivals to the shrine before leaving through the south gate. Tang Soryeong had used it to lure away herb gatherers. Gwi Sam had made a hundred taels of silver selling nothing but false versions.

The seven had never trusted one another. As a result, Ryu Dan had inherited thirteen locations for opportunities that did not exist.

Bells clanged furiously in the direction of the shrine.

The three factions appeared to have met.

“The Black Tooth Gang heard of the thousand-year elixir first!”

“You can’t even read your half of the map!”

“Both of you stand aside! The Iron Fist School will inspect it!”

Their shouts overlapped in the distance.

Ryu Dan crossed to the opposite side of the official road. While everyone who might follow him seized one another by the collar over an empty treasure chest, the real opportunities would quietly change hands.

His first destination was the dry well behind Return Inn.

It was dark even at noon. Ryu Dan climbed down a rope and pressed the seventh brick up from the bottom. A stone panel slid open, revealing a narrow hollow.

A pair of black cloth shoes waited inside.

Plum-Shadow Steps.

They were the shoes the qinggong master Mae Hwajin had used for training. Twelve thin plates of dark iron lay in each sole, making each shoe as heavy as a sack of rice. Mae Hwajin had moved the hiding place three times to keep the others from stealing them, but from loop 8,106 onward, she had used this well.

Ryu Dan changed shoes at the bottom.

His knees buckled the moment he stood.

“They really are heavy.”

Mae Hwajin had run over mountain peaks in them. Ryu Dan’s body had only just gained its first thread of qi. The difference between knowing and doing nailed both his ankles to the earth.

He braced a hand on the wall and took one step. The iron plates struck the ground.

His ankle wobbled on the second. On the third, he aligned knee and toes. Only on the tenth did he lift the shoe without dragging it.

Then he looked up.

There was one way out: the rope.

“An opportunity should include delivery.”

He complained, but he was smiling. Training he had only ever watched on someone else’s body now hung from his own feet.

He climbed one section at a time. Whenever the shoes dragged him down, he wrapped the thin qi opened by the Meridian-Cleansing Pill around his ankles. He did not try to lift the weight by force. He reduced the sway.

By the time he crawled over the lip, sweat had soaked his clothes.

But his last step was more precise than his first.

His first prize was a vessel for footwork.

The second waited inside the Green Snake Apothecary.

The shop was unusually quiet. Its owner and guards had run to the shrine after the false treasure, leaving only a drowsing apprentice.

Ryu Dan entered carrying a paper bundle.

“Urgent medicinal goods from the mountain shrine.”

“Did our elders already find it?”

“I didn’t see them. People who smell treasure rarely look behind them.”

The apprentice took the bundle to the storeroom. It contained nothing but dried mugwort.

Ryu Dan opened the fourth drawer from the right and slid aside its thin false bottom.

Click.

A transparent jade bead rolled out. Black mist floated inside it in fine threads.

The Myriad-Poison Ward Bead.

Tang Soryeong sought it first whenever she began her study of poisons. The bead drew in toxic qi and exposed its nature. Combined with her detoxification method, it could even extract poison already inside a body.

The moment Ryu Dan tucked it away, someone collapsed in the front room.

“Help! Someone help him!”

A merchant in silk convulsed on the apothecary floor. His lips were blue, and purple stained the beds of his nails. Beside him, an elderly apothecary shook a celadon bottle.

Apparently one employee had not gone to the shrine.

“Mushroom poisoning. This antidote will save him!” The old man held out a hand to the merchant’s servant. “Five taels of silver.”

“I don’t have it here! Save him first!”

“No money, no medicine.”

The convulsions worsened. Onlookers crowded around, but no one moved.

Ryu Dan lifted the merchant’s eyelids and checked beneath his tongue.

Not mushroom poison. Sleeping Meridian Toxin, which the Green Snake Apothecary mixed in tiny amounts with cheap joint medicine. Today they had either measured badly or deliberately induced an acute attack.

“That antidote is fake.”

The old apothecary glared. “What would an inn boy know?”

“Take a sip, then.” Ryu Dan pointed at the bottle.

The man’s hand stopped.

The spectators saw that brief hesitation.

“You called it an antidote.”

“Why won’t you drink it?”

Ryu Dan took the bottle and let one drop fall into an empty cup. When he brought the Myriad-Poison Ward Bead near, the mist inside churned violently. The liquid in the cup turned purple, and black crystals as fine as hair formed at the bottom.

An herb merchant sucked in a breath. “That’s poison.”

“They were selling poison as the antidote?”

The old man backed away. “A trick! That bastard mixed something in!”

“Then you can prove it by drinking.” Ryu Dan offered him the cup.

The apothecary shut his mouth.

The merchant’s breathing thinned almost to nothing. Exposing a swindle was not a victory if the victim died.

Ryu Dan ran to the medicine drawers. Two qian of coptis. Half a qian of licorice. One qian of whiteflower root. Tang Soryeong had failed 312 times to detoxify this poison. On the 313th attempt, she decocted the coptis first. In loop 2,005, she discovered that the whiteflower root had to be added last for the medicine to retain its effect.

Ryu Dan ground the herbs and mixed them into hot water.

“Force his mouth open.”

The servant seized the merchant’s jaw. Ryu Dan poured in half the decoction and placed the Ward Bead below his solar plexus. Its black mist whirled.

Purple threads moved beneath the merchant’s skin, toxic qi drawing toward the bead. Following Tang Soryeong’s method, Ryu Dan pressed three points in sequence between the man’s ribs.

At the first, his body jerked.

At the second, he vomited black blood.

At the third, his blocked breath burst free.

“Gasp!”

The merchant rolled over and coughed deeply. Color returned to his blue lips.

The crowd’s muttering became cheers.

“He’s alive!”

“The apothecary demanded five taels, and the inn boy saved him on the spot!”

The servant dropped to his knees before Ryu Dan. “Benefactor, may I ask your name?”

“Ryu Dan of Return Inn.”

“Didn’t he say he was an inn boy?” someone asked.

Ryu Dan looked at the old man. “Apparently I’m a better apothecary than the apothecary.”

Laughter erupted.

The old man tried to flee through the back. The revived merchant gave an order in a hoarse voice.

“Take him. Hand him to the magistrate. Make him confess everything he put in me.”

The servants twisted the old man’s arms behind him. The onlookers who had pushed an inn boy aside now opened a path for Ryu Dan. Several asked for directions to Return Inn.

One treatment had ended a fraud and earned him a name—and customers.

Heat gathered around Ryu Dan’s dantian.

The flow inside the Ward Bead joined with the detoxification method he had observed from Tang Soryeong. The colors of poisons, their paths through the body, and the order in which to drive them out descended from knowledge into sensation at his fingertips.

[Third star node of the Meridian of Ten Thousand Rehearsals recognizes its foundation.]

[Myriad-Poison Ward Bead and detoxification method: integration in progress.]

The node had not opened completely. He still had to clear the last remnant of poison.

With the bead in his robe, Ryu Dan headed for the third location.

Gwi Sam, the Beggars’ Union regressor, had hidden his silver beneath a noodle shop in the market. Order a bowl and tap the table three times with the chopsticks, then the bowl twice, and the proprietor would provide the key to the privy.

When Ryu Dan did exactly that, the man narrowed his eyes.

“Who told you?”

“A guest with large ears.”

Gwi Sam’s ears had been enormous. The proprietor asked nothing more and tossed over the key.

A hollow log lay under the floor of the firewood shed beside the privy. Ryu Dan removed its plug. Ten taels of silver and two ciphered messages spilled out.

More than enough to repay the principal of two taels.

He bit one of the ingots.

“You’ll break a tooth doing that,” the proprietor said behind him.

“I wanted to know if it was real.”

“It is. Dentistry costs extra.”

“Are you acquainted with the owner of Return Inn?”

“People who count money all resemble one another.”

Ryu Dan packed the silver and messages away.

Three harvests. Plum-Shadow Steps expanded what his body might become. The Ward Bead had saved a life. Gwi Sam’s money could restore the place where Ryu Dan lived.

Dust still rose above the mountain shrine.

The three owners of the false opportunity continued fighting over an empty stone chest. The paper Ryu Dan had placed inside read, THE ONE WHO YIELDS TO THE OTHERS IS THE TRUE MASTER.

It seemed no true master had arrived.

When Ryu Dan returned to the inn, Ma Yeongsun stood behind the counter with the debt papers spread before her. The village headman and a legal scribe sat beside her, just as Ryu Dan had requested.

“You’re late,” she said.

“When will the Black Tooth Gang arrive?”

“Within half an hour. They’re bringing the man who plans to take possession.”

Ryu Dan placed two taels of silver on the debt note. “The principal.”

The headman stroked his beard. “The Black Tooth Gang will refuse to accept only that.”

“The original document names two taels and nothing else. The seal on the interest note is different.”

The scribe held both pages to the light. One carried Ma Yeongsun’s seal. The other carried an imitation in ink.

“It is forged.”

Ryu Dan had seen the papers during 418 threats. Every time the thug slammed them onto a table, Ryu Dan memorized another flaw—even the tiny nick in the seal.

The headman wrote a receipt for the principal and stamped it with the official seal. “If the creditor refuses payment, depositing the sum with the magistrate discharges the debt. The lien is released.”

Ma Yeongsun set the inn’s old deed beside the new certificate. After her husband’s death, a debt of two taels had made Return Inn half the gang’s property. Now it belonged entirely to her again.

“Mine,” she said.

That one word was enough for Ryu Dan.

“It always was.”

“What is mine and what other people acknowledge as mine are different things.”

She tucked the certificate into the innermost fold of her robe, then eyed the remaining silver.

“Is that mine too?”

“No.”

“Pity.”

The door flew open hard enough to threaten its new hinges.

Six Black Tooth thugs stormed in after finding nothing at the shrine. The scarred man they had chased from the inn led them. Dirt and chicken feathers covered their clothes, evidence of a fight with the feral dogs inside the shrine’s stone chest.

“Ryu Dan!”

“Did you find the elixir?”

“That was your doing?”

“The note said you’d find it if you yielded to one another.”

One thug glanced at the man beside him. “I said we should yield.”

“You smashed the chest first!”

“Shut up!” the leader roared. “Hand over the inn papers!”

Ma Yeongsun spread the official certificate on the counter. “The debt is paid. The inn is mine.”

“On whose authority?”

The headman rose. “The law’s.”

The thug’s momentum faltered. Then he looked at the five men behind him and the spectators outside. If he returned empty-handed, his own gang would beat him.

He drew a club. “Law or no law, break everything!”

The first club swung at the counter.

Ryu Dan stepped in front of it wearing the weighted shoes.

His feet were heavy—heavier than they had felt in the well. There he had moved slowly. Now he had to pass between six attacks.

The club fell.

Ryu Dan tried to evade, but his foot lagged half a beat. He blocked with his tray, and the shock rang through his arm. The second man’s fist grazed his ribs.

“Hah!” The thug laughed. “You had tricks earlier. Now you can’t even drag your own shoes!”

He was right.

Ryu Dan did not take them off.

The problem was not the shoes. It was that he still saw their weight only as an enemy. Mae Hwajin had never tried to lift Plum-Shadow Steps. She turned the falling weight into force for the next step.

Ryu Dan sent his first thread of qi into his ankles.

Instead of lifting his foot, he rolled his heel.

The dark iron pitched forward. His body slid beneath the club.

The first thug lost his target and struck the third across the shoulder.

“Agh! Why’d you hit me?”

“He dodged!”

Ryu Dan passed between them. His right foot stamped, the weight anchoring it to the floor, and his left pivoted around that fixed point. The second thug’s fist landed on the fourth man’s jaw.

Thud.

“Two,” Ryu Dan said.

“What are you counting?”

The fifth and sixth charged together. Ryu Dan stepped into the gap. They tried to stop too late. Planted by Plum-Shadow Steps, he did not sway; the men brushed his shoulders and cracked their foreheads together.

Bang.

They sat down side by side.

“Four.”

The onlookers laughed and took up the count.

“Five is the one punched in the jaw!”

“Then the one hit in the shoulder makes six!”

Only the leader remained. More precisely, all six were still upright, but after striking one another, none wanted to attack first.

Ryu Dan stepped forward.

This time the shoes did not drag behind. Their iron became a firm axis beneath his feet. The second star around his dantian blazed open.

[Second star node of the Meridian of Ten Thousand Rehearsals opened.]

[Weight utilization of Plum-Shadow Steps integrated.]

The leader backed into a chair and fell. He grabbed a table, which tipped and swept the other five down in a row.

Crash!

All six rolled across the floor in one tangled heap.

Ma Yeongsun opened her ledger immediately. “One chair, two tables, three plates.”

“We didn’t break them!”

“You lay down in my inn and they broke.”

“That inn boy—”

“Ryu Dan is standing perfectly well.”

There was no answer to that.

The spectators began wagering on who would pay for the furniture first. The headman announced that the magistrate’s report would include both assault and destruction of property.

At last, the thugs emptied their pockets. Coins and clipped bits of silver piled on the counter. Ma Yeongsun even priced the scratches on a table leg.

“That scratch was already there!”

“Can you prove you saw it before?”

“This is robbery!”

“Then stop falling down in other people’s homes.”

The six left without money, without the debt note, and with faces battered by one another. The crowd outside refused to clear a path, forcing them to squeeze out in single file.

Only after they disappeared did Ryu Dan rub his side.

“Did they hit you?” Ma Yeongsun asked.

“Grazed me.”

“I should have added treatment costs to the furniture bill.”

“Does the ledger come before me?”

“You need a ledger before you can buy medicine.”

Since this was true, Ryu Dan said nothing.

He took out the remaining detoxification brew. A faint remnant of the merchant’s poison still clouded the Ward Bead. Following the last sequence of Tang Soryeong’s method, he submerged the bead in the liquid.

Black qi seeped out and was neutralized in the medicinal liquid. A tiny point of starlight formed at the center of the clear bead.

The third star around his dantian answered.

[Third star node of the Meridian of Ten Thousand Rehearsals opened.]

[Myriad-Poison Ward Bead and detoxification method integrated.]

Ryu Dan touched the rim of a water cup. He could distinguish the faint smell of lime in the plain water, the lingering herbs he had handled, and the medicinal qi of the Meridian-Cleansing Pill in his blood. He had gained more than the power to remove poison. He could now sense unfamiliar substances flowing inside and outside his body.

In one day he had claimed three opportunities, saved a life, and reclaimed the inn. Two sealed stars had opened.

But Ryu Dan did not stop to savor it.

Gwi Sam’s messages remained to be decoded.

Before the night grew deep, he sat at one end of the counter and spread out both sheets. The Beggars’ Union cipher was not read through letters, but through grease stains and the number of folds. Ryu Dan had once watched a drunken Gwi Sam solve it in loop 4,400.

The first message contained the schedule for the Azure Peak Sect’s selection trial.

When Ryu Dan held the blank spaces of the second over the lamplight, a hidden pocket appeared. Something thin had been sealed between two layers of paper.

He split the edge.

A blue jade plaque and a folded note fell out.

A sword dividing clouds was carved on one face of the plaque. On the other was Baek Mugyeol’s name.

The Azure Peak Token—the identity token of the sect’s sword prodigy.

It should have been inside the Tomb of Seven Returns with the dead regressors. Ryu Dan did not know why it had been hidden among Gwi Sam’s silver.

The note was stranger.

He stopped breathing at the first character.

It was his handwriting. The strokes leaned slightly right, just as they did when he wrote figures in the corner of the inn ledger. Even his habit of shortening the final downstroke in the character for day was the same.

But Ryu Dan had never written this note.

There was only one line.

—On the thirty-first day, go to the Azure Peak Sect.

He turned the paper over. Nothing. Neither flame nor water revealed another word.

Day thirty-one had existed for the first time today.

No one should have known it.

Yet a Ryu Dan from a day that had never existed had left directions for the Ryu Dan of today.

The cloud-dividing sword on the Azure Peak Token gleamed coldly beneath the lamp.
