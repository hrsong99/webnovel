# Chapter 3. I Have Seen That Technique Nine Thousand Times

Ryu Dan was stopped three times at the gates of the Azure Peak Sect.

The first time was because of his clothes.

“Deliveries go through the rear gate,” said the disciple on guard.

“I’m here for the selection trial.”

“Spectators use the west gate.”

Ryu Dan reached into his robe and produced the Azure Peak Token.

A sword dividing clouds had been engraved on the face of the scratched blue-jade token. Only direct disciples of the sect received one. Baek Mugyeol had once treasured this token more than his life. After the thirty-six-hundredth loop, he had started using it to prop open doors.

It would be new again on the first day anyway, he had said.

Today, the token had not reset.

The gate disciple’s face hardened at the sight of it.

That was the second time Ryu Dan was stopped.

“That belongs to Senior Uncle Baek.”

“I know.”

“How did you get it?”

“He gave it to me.”

“Who did?”

“Baek Mugyeol.”

It wasn’t a lie.

During the 8,052nd loop, Baek Mugyeol had tossed the blood-smeared token onto Ryu Dan’s serving tray.

*If I die, it’s yours. Don’t spend it on soup in the next life.*

The promise had vanished when the day rewound. Ryu Dan was the only person who remembered it.

But a gift was a gift.

The disciple gripped his sword.

“Senior Uncle Baek disappeared on the thirtieth day. We haven’t even found his body.”

“I know that too.”

“And now a servant from an inn appears with his personal token and claims he received it from the man himself?”

The applicants nearby edged away. A lowly servant stealing a precious token was one thing. A lowly servant murdering its owner for it was another.

Ryu Dan did not explain. They would not believe that Baek Mugyeol had died inside the Tomb of Seven Returns. If he revealed the tomb’s location, the road ahead would be wet with blood before noon.

Then the third person came to stop him.

“Take your hand off your sword.”

A young man in a dark blue martial uniform approached from within the gates. He looked barely past thirty, with a face browned by the sun and thirteen small clouds embroidered along one sleeve.

Nam Sogun, an examiner of the Azure Peak Sect.

He took the token from Ryu Dan and tested its weight. He ran a thumbnail over an old sword nick along the edge, then tilted it toward the gate lamp to inspect the inscription within.

“It’s genuine.”

“Then shouldn’t we arrest him?” the gate disciple asked.

“We can investigate a suspect after detaining him,” Nam Sogun said. “But if we seize an innocent man, there is no way to give him back the shame we took from him.”

He looked directly at Ryu Dan.

“I’ll hold the token. The selection trial will tell me whether your story deserves further hearing. Run, and I record you as a thief. Pass but fail to explain its source, and your place will remain unconfirmed. Fair?”

Ryu Dan nodded.

There was reason to suspect him, and Nam Sogun suspected him only as far as the evidence allowed. He had not drawn steel on an inn servant simply because he wanted Baek Mugyeol back.

That was fair enough.

“I’ll take the trial.”

The entrance examination began with strength.

Ryu Dan stood before a black stone taller than a man. Hundreds of palm marks had been stamped into its surface.

“If you have internal energy, circulate it,” said the examiner beside it. “If not, strike with everything you have.”

The watching applicants stared at Ryu Dan’s waist. He carried neither sword nor saber. Only the old cleaning rag from Return Inn hung from his belt.

Ryu Dan closed his fist. The bruise along his ribs, left by the earlier glancing fist, pulled tight.

His first star node had settled learned movements into his body. His second and third had opened his breathing and sharpened his senses. None of that had conjured new muscle onto his arms or filled his dantian with a lifetime of power.

What he could produce now was the strength of a twenty-year-old inn servant.

Boom.

A faint crack near the bottom of the strength stone lit up.

The examiner wrote on a wooden tally.

“Lower grade, Class B.”

Laughter leaked from behind him.

“Class B?”

“Shouldn’t he be taking a firewood-chopping test?”

Ryu Dan opened his fist and examined his reddened knuckles. The result was exactly what he had expected. If he stamped like Mae Hwajin, his ankle would snap. If he swung like Baek Mugyeol, his shoulder would leave its socket. His memories held 9,431 lifetimes, but his body had opened only three doors.

A low result was not an insult.

It was his present location.

“Next.”

When Ryu Dan stepped aside without protest, the laughter quickly faded. There was little pleasure in mocking a weak man who made no false claims to strength.

The second testing ground lay beneath a long tiled eave. Seven bronze bells hung from a beam, each tied to a cord of a different length. As two examiners turned handles at either side, the bells traveled through grooves overhead—some fast, some slow, some swaying left and right. Broken pieces of wooden swords littered the floor beneath them.

“This is the Trial of Seven Chimes,” said the middle-aged martial artist in charge. “Choose one fragment from the floor. Throw it once. Ring three bells to pass. Five earns first class. Ring all seven, and an elder of the Azure Peak Sect will personally review your name.”

The first applicant selected a fragment no longer than two finger joints. It struck the slowest bell and fell.

*Chime.*

The next applicant packed internal energy into a broad splinter. It grazed two bells and broke against a third.

*Chime. Chime. Chime.*

“Three. Third class. Pass.”

Then it was Ryu Dan’s turn.

He did not look at the wood. He watched the bells.

They seemed to move independently, but every one of them ran on the same axle. When the second bell reached its leftmost point, the fourth slowed. When the sixth turned, the first bell’s cord rose half an inch. The Trial of Seven Chimes tested observation more than sleight of hand.

But the seventh bell’s cord had been replaced that morning. Its rhythm no longer matched the other six. Nam Sogun had changed it to catch anyone who had memorized an old solution.

Ryu Dan chose the ugliest piece on the floor—a thin wedge broken from a wooden sword’s grip. Dried blood made one side rough. The other had been worn smooth.

“You intend to use that?” the examiner asked.

“The broken one is better.”

“Why?”

“It’s honest about where it will bounce.”

Ryu Dan held the wedge between two fingers. He counted the examiners’ breathing, the sleeves stirring in the breeze, and the lingering note of each bell.

One.

Two.

The seventh bell slipped alone outside the pattern.

Three.

He threw.

The wedge sailed past the nearest bell. Someone in the crowd snorted too soon.

Then the distant fourth bell swung back and caught it.

*Chime.*

The wedge rebounded upward and struck the underside of the second.

*Chime.*

Its note pushed the connected cord, delaying the third bell by half an inch. The wedge slipped through that opening and clipped the sixth and first in succession.

*Chime. Chime.*

Four.

Nearly all its speed was gone. But the first bell returned and nudged its smooth face. The wood skimmed low, rang the fifth bell, bounced once off the floor, and touched the third.

*Chime. Chime.*

Six.

The seventh bell waited at the far end.

The wedge ran out of force and dropped.

A groan of disappointment passed beneath the eaves. Six was already a record, but Ryu Dan had lowered his hand.

On the floor, the wedge spun like a top. Its blood-roughened face caught against the stone while its smooth face continued to slide. The slowing tip tapped the rim of the seventh bell just as it returned.

*Chime.*

Seven notes gathered into a single line of sound overhead.

Ryu Dan stood beneath them. His second node had put weight into his feet. His third had let him perceive the links between moving things. He had not used a grain of internal energy.

The fragment’s strength had never mattered.

What mattered was the instant the seven bells moved one another.

The examiner looked from his tally to the bells.

“Seven Chimes... first class.”

“Didn’t you say an elder would personally review his name?” someone called.

The examiner cleared his throat. “Seven Chimes, first class, with personal review by an elder.”

The applicants who had laughed at the strength stone went silent. Lower-grade Class B strength, yet precision enough to ring every bell. Together, those results did not look weak.

They looked strange.

A single clap sounded from across the courtyard.

Nam Sogun crouched beneath the bells and examined the knots, then the wedge on the floor. He knew better than anyone that the seventh cord had been changed.

“Did you calculate the seventh?”

“When I threw.”

“And after the piece began spinning on the floor?”

“I threw it rough side down.”

“There would still have been drift.”

“That’s why I waited for the wind to stop.”

Every answer joined an observation to an action. Nam Sogun did not belittle the result as luck, and Ryu Dan did not dress it up as a mystical feat.

“Could you do it again?” Nam Sogun asked.

“If you change the bells.”

“Why?”

“Otherwise you’ll think I memorized it.”

The corner of Nam Sogun’s mouth lifted slightly.

“Then instead of changing the bells, we’ll change the target.”

He took a wooden sword from the weapons rack.

“Hall Master, you mean to test him yourself?” the other examiner asked.

“His strength is lower-grade Class B. I won’t use internal energy.”

“But you’ll be using the Thirteen Falling Clouds.”

“That is why I can stop before he gets hurt.”

Nam Sogun did not boast that he was strong. He counted the skill required not to injure his opponent as part of his own responsibility.

Applicants crowded into a ring. Even those waiting at the strength stone came over. Word had already crossed the wall: an inn servant who had rung seven bells was about to duel an examiner.

Nam Sogun offered him a wooden sword.

Ryu Dan shook his head and held up the same broken wedge.

“I’ll use this.”

“You don’t know how to hold a sword?”

“I do. My wrist would tire before the rest of me.”

One of the applicants who had laughed earlier stared. Given the perfect chance to pretend at greatness, Ryu Dan had admitted his limit first.

Nam Sogun lowered his sword at an angle.

“One condition. Touch me once with that wedge before the Thirteen Falling Clouds ends, and you win. Fail by the final form, and I win.”

“Thirteen forms will be enough.”

“Confident.”

“No.” Ryu Dan raised the wedge. “I’m confident I can make you use all thirteen before I win.”

Whispers burst around the ring.

Nam Sogun’s gaze cooled—not with anger, but with the focus of a man who had finally found something worth testing.

“Come.”

Ryu Dan spoke first.

“First Form. Clouds Part the Road.”

Nam Sogun’s sword moved.

The horizontal cut opened the space between them like clouds parting before a road. Ryu Dan retreated half a step. The wind of the blade brushed his lapel.

“Second Form. Falling Rain Scatters the Peaks.”

Three afterimages spilled from the wooden sword. Ryu Dan ignored the center and watched the direction of Nam Sogun’s right knee. He raised the wedge.

*Tak.*

The fragment glanced along the sword’s flat and shed the impact. His palm went numb. If he had tried to block with force, his fingers would have broken.

“Third Form. Cloud River Crosses the Sky.”

The sword tip sank, then surged upward.

Ryu Dan was already moving into the empty space on the left.

Three forms ended.

Nam Sogun did not stop.

“Fourth Form,” Ryu Dan called before him. “Drifting Clouds Sever the Ridge.”

The blade swept toward his shoulder. Ryu Dan turned with a lateral Plum-Shadow Step. The iron plates in his weighted shoes pinned his center. It was Mae Hwajin’s long stride, cut down to fit the body he had now.

The Fifth Form followed. Then the Sixth.

Each name left Ryu Dan’s mouth before Nam Sogun’s sword completed its opening motion.

No one in the courtyard was breathing loudly now.

On the Seventh Form, Nam Sogun changed the sequence. He inserted the Ninth Form’s thrust into the Seventh’s place—a variation that would pierce anyone reciting the orthodox order from memory.

Before the sword tip moved, Ryu Dan said, “Ninth Form. Autumn Clouds Enter the Valley. You switched to your left foot.”

He planted the wedge against the ground and slid sideways. The wooden sword passed through an empty sleeve.

Nam Sogun’s expression changed for the first time.

“Who taught you?”

His sword did not pause.

“You did.”

“I have never seen you before.”

“I’ve seen you many times.”

The Tenth Form followed. Then the Eleventh. Again, Ryu Dan named each one before Nam Sogun’s sword moved.

His breathing grew ragged.

The third star node had opened his lungs, not replaced his stamina. Blood seeped between his fingers where the splinter had torn his palm.

Nam Sogun noticed. The next cut slowed by half an inch—not as mercy, but to make each beginning and ending clearer, so Ryu Dan could not mistake changing speed for prediction.

He would test the mystery to the end.

“Twelfth Form. Heaven’s Clouds Overturn the Sea.”

The wooden sword fell from overhead.

Ryu Dan did not retreat. He angled the wedge and entered beneath the force. His wrist rang. His shoulder screamed. But his foot reached the outside of Nam Sogun’s right foot.

Only the thirteenth remained.

Nam Sogun’s sword momentum caught for the smallest instant.

No spectator saw it.

Ryu Dan had seen it 2,117 times.

While Baek Mugyeol stole, broke apart, and rebuilt the Thirteen Falling Clouds across countless loops, he had entered through this same seam again and again. At the end of the Twelfth Form, Nam Sogun gripped the earth with his right big toe to recover his power. That delayed his hip by half a beat and raised his right shoulder half an inch.

“Thirteenth Form,” Ryu Dan said. “Ten Thousand Clouds Return to the Mountain.”

The sword folded thirteen layers of cloud into one descending strike.

Ryu Dan opened his shoulder as if he meant to go left. Nam Sogun’s eyes followed. In that instant, Ryu Dan loaded all the weight of his iron shoe into his right foot and slipped his unweighted left step inside the sword.

The wooden blade grazed his shoulder. Cloth tore. A hot line opened across his skin.

But the broken wedge rested against Nam Sogun’s throat.

Ryu Dan had touched first.

Nam Sogun stopped. Ryu Dan swallowed his rough breathing and relaxed his hand. The wood did not press into the man’s skin. A touch had won the duel. Anything more was waste.

Silence held for a long moment.

Then Nam Sogun lowered his sword.

“You won.”

The courtyard erupted.

A man with lower-grade Class B strength had read every one of an Azure Peak examiner’s Thirteen Falling Clouds and placed broken wood against his throat before the final form landed. Luck could explain neither the seven bells nor thirteen predictions.

Ryu Dan lowered the wedge.

“May I add one thing?”

“The victor offers advice too?”

“It will sting less coming from someone after you have beaten him.”

Laughter broke the tension. It was no longer directed at Ryu Dan.

Even Nam Sogun smiled. “Say it.”

Ryu Dan pointed at his right foot.

“Don’t force your shoulder down after the Twelfth Form. The shoulder isn’t the cause. Your big toe is. You grip the ground, so your hip arrives late.”

Nam Sogun’s smile vanished.

A stranger had named the obstruction he had failed to correct for years. He did not believe merely because Ryu Dan had spoken.

“I’ll verify it.”

He went to a wooden training dummy at the courtyard’s edge and executed the sequence through the Twelfth Form. At the final transition, he released his right big toe and moved his center to the middle of the sole.

“Thirteenth Form. Ten Thousand Clouds Return to the Mountain.”

*Crack!*

The wooden sword split the dummy’s shoulder and buried itself in its chest. No sword aura had been used, yet a long fracture ran down the seasoned elm torso. The half-inch tremor was gone.

Nam Sogun studied the broken weapon.

“It works.”

Two words.

To the Azure Peak disciples, they mattered more than the duel. Young though he was, Nam Sogun ranked among the sect’s finest practitioners of the Thirteen Falling Clouds. He had tested an inn servant’s correction before everyone and acknowledged its effect.

The crowd’s judgment changed again.

Ryu Dan had not won by accident.

He could look at a supreme technique and improve it.

Nam Sogun returned and offered his hand. It was not the hand of a man who had abandoned every suspicion. It was the hand of one who recognized ability while keeping the remaining questions intact.

Ryu Dan took it.

“Ryu Dan,” Nam Sogun announced. “Lower-grade Class B in strength. Class A in Seven Chimes. Class A in combat.”

The record keeper dipped his brush in haste.

“What is his overall placement?”

Nam Sogun considered Ryu Dan. On ability, there was no question. But the Azure Peak Token of the missing Baek Mugyeol was still unexplained.

“Provisional first.”

The courtyard stirred again.

“Provisional,” Nam Sogun continued. “His first-place marker will be attached to a pending seat. Before dawn, he joins our survey of the outer Tomb of Seven Returns. If his ability and the token’s origin survive examination, his place as first disciple of the Outer Hall will be confirmed.”

It was neither acceptance nor rejection. There had never been a position called provisional first in the Azure Peak Sect. Nam Sogun had refused to diminish achievement for the sake of suspicion, and refused to forget suspicion for the sake of achievement.

A blue cord was tied to a bronze plaque. Clouds marked its front. Four characters on the back read *provisional first*.

When Ryu Dan fastened it to his belt, the surrounding looks changed. Moments earlier, he had been an unarmed inn servant. Now he was the man who had ranked first in the selection trial and corrected an examiner’s supreme technique.

Even the gate disciple approached with Baek Mugyeol’s token held in both hands. He passed it to Nam Sogun rather than returning it, but he addressed Ryu Dan differently.

“You should have that shoulder treated, First... Candidate.”

The last word came awkwardly.

Ryu Dan smiled. “Does a candidate pay half price for medicine?”

“Ask the Medicine Hall.”

One door had opened.

After the trials, Nam Sogun summoned Ryu Dan to the Inner Hall archive. A white-haired elder waited at a table. Ryu Dan’s note had been sealed between two thin panes of glass.

*On the thirty-first day, go to the Azure Peak Sect.*

The handwriting was unmistakably his. Yet he had never written it. He could not have written about a day that had never existed.

The elder held it to the lamp.

“You said this is your handwriting?”

“Yes. Even the stroke habits match.”

“No.”

Ryu Dan looked up.

“What do you mean?”

“No human wrote this with a brush.”

With a fine needle, the elder pointed to the first stroke of Ryu Dan’s surname. Inside what looked like a line of ink ran a chain of dots so small the eye could barely catch them.

Seven dots. Then seven more.

Every stroke repeated the same pattern.

“Old formations borrow the memory of a nearby mind when they leave energy behind,” the elder said. “They engrave a record in a shape the viewer can recognize. That is why you saw your own hand.”

Nam Sogun leaned closer. “Do you know the formation?”

The elder joined seven hidden dots beneath the message. They curved into the shape of seven stars and a handle.

“The Seven-Star Return Formation.”

Cold passed down Ryu Dan’s spine.

Beneath Return Inn.

The floor he had wiped again and again while shedding blood.

These were the same lines that had burned at the heart of the formation.

“This is not someone imitating your handwriting,” the elder said. The message behind the glass reddened in the lamplight. “The return formation itself used your memory to leave you a record.”

Ryu Dan had thought the seven dead regressors were waiting for him.

He had been wrong.

The formation that had sent them back 9,431 times was calling its surviving recording vessel into the first-ever thirty-first day.
