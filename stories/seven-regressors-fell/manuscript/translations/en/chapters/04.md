# Chapter 4. The Geniuses’ Secret Vault

Twelve people entered the outer Tomb of Seven Returns.

Originally, only nine came back out.

Ryu Dan studied the split boulder on the mountainside and revised the number.

Today, all twelve would return.

“Why is the guide walking in front?” asked Jin Hu, the runner-up in the selection trial. He was the young man who had laughed at Ryu Dan’s strength-stone result.

He was no longer laughing. He had checked the *provisional first* plaque at Ryu Dan’s belt three separate times.

“Because he knows the way,” Nam Sogun answered.

“He still hasn’t explained how he knows where the tomb is.”

“That is why I’m behind him. If he’s wrong, I seize him. If he’s right, I follow.”

Suspicion and ability, counted separately.

Ryu Dan slipped a hand into a crack in the boulder. When he pressed the seventh groove, the mountainside split open and breathed cold air.

“Right so far,” Nam Sogun said, drawing his sword.

He returned Baek Mugyeol’s Azure Peak Token to Ryu Dan.

“The man who opened the road should carry it. We’ll finish examining its origin after we come out.”

The stone door sealed behind the party. In the darkness, seven bronze lines in the floor lit one after another.

Ryu Dan knew this path. The regressors had died hundreds of times in these outer passages alone. After that, they had crossed them with their eyes closed.

The bandage from the Medicine Hall pulled at Ryu Dan’s shoulder whenever he moved. A reminder of his duel with Nam Sogun.

He knew the first trap too.

Ryu Dan stopped.

“Jin Hu. Don’t lift your right foot.”

Jin Hu’s heel hovered half an inch above the floor.

“Why?”

“Needles will fire from the ceiling.”

“But I already stepped on it.”

“Stepping down is safe. Lifting off kills you.”

Every disciple looked up. There were no visible holes. Sweat gathered on Jin Hu’s brow.

Ryu Dan removed one of his Plum-Shadow weighted shoes and set it on the pressure plate.

“Now move.”

Jin Hu pulled his foot free. The iron-heavy shoe kept the plate depressed while Ryu Dan broke a bronze line in the wall with his thumbnail and reclaimed it.

A black needle dropped from the ceiling.

Its tip melted a pit into the stone.

Jin Hu swallowed.

“You could have warned me earlier.”

“Would you have stepped on it if I had?”

“No.”

“Then now was early enough.”

A short laugh came from the rear. Jin Hu did not get angry. Instead, he began watching where Ryu Dan put his feet.

That was the look of a man who had survived once.

The second trap was a door painted on the wall.

Three blue doors shone side by side at the blocked end of the passage. *Life* was written over the center. *Death* marked the two beside it.

A young disciple headed for the middle.

Ryu Dan caught him by the back of his collar.

“You trust the writing?”

“You want me to take a death door when the life door is right there?”

“Yes.”

Ryu Dan held the Myriad-Poison Ward Bead near the left death door. The black mist inside the clear bead remained still. When he moved it toward the life door, the mist boiled.

“The life door is full of Bone-Melting Poison. Open it and everyone behind you dissolves too.”

Nam Sogun tapped the threshold with his scabbard. Something beyond the middle door scratched like hundreds of insects.

“Then the left is the true path?” he asked.

“No.”

Ryu Dan pushed the hinge of the right death door. Instead of the door, an entire section of wall revolved to reveal a hidden passage.

“The left door has no poison. It only opens over a cliff.”

The disciple closed his mouth. Ryu Dan did not mock him. Two loops before the regressors solved this place, Ryu Dan himself had opened the life door and died.

Traps fed more readily on certainty than ignorance.

The third lay in a broad stone chamber.

At its center rested a blue-jade tablet identical to Baek Mugyeol’s Azure Peak Token.

The disciples quickened their pace.

“That belongs to Senior Uncle Baek!”

“Stop.”

This time, every one of them stopped.

Two rescues had given weight to Ryu Dan’s voice.

He drew the genuine token from his robe and held it up. The false token on the floor flashed blue. Blades shot from all four walls.

There was nothing to cut.

All twelve people stood outside the threshold.

The blades crossed the empty chamber three times and withdrew. The final sweep split the false token itself. Red liquid, thick as blood, spilled from inside.

Nam Sogun crouched to inspect it.

“A formation fluid that lures people with the scent of blood. The first man through would have been pushed in by everyone behind him.”

He turned to the expedition.

“Record merit for the outer-tomb survey. Ryu Dan disabled the first three traps and saved every member of the party, Jin Hu included.”

A public declaration lasted longer than a whisper.

In the disciples’ eyes, the word *provisional* was already fading from Ryu Dan’s first-place plaque. Jin Hu moved directly behind him.

“Why are you back there?” Ryu Dan asked.

“So I stop when you stop.”

“You learn quickly.”

“You think just anyone places second?”

That much pride could be put to use.

Beyond the chamber waited seven branching passages.

Ryu Dan chose the second path from the right.

This was where the problem began.

In 317 loops, the senior Azure Peak disciple Gwak Hyeon had always lit signal incense at the next corner. An ambush force outside would invert the formation, and the floor would split beneath the three people in front. Gwak Hyeon had betrayed the sect for money, for his family, and for revenge. The reason changed. The habit did not: his right thumb pushed the cap off the incense tube.

The corner approached.

Without looking back, Ryu Dan reached out.

*Snap.*

He caught Gwak Hyeon’s right wrist. A black incense tube fell from the disciple’s sleeve.

Nam Sogun’s sword touched Gwak Hyeon’s throat in the same instant.

“Explain.”

The man’s face drained white.

“I—I...”

“You were going to signal through the northern vent,” Ryu Dan said, picking up the tube. “Invert the formation after the lines blink three times.”

Confusion appeared in Gwak Hyeon’s eyes before fear.

“How do you know the order?”

“Who gave it?”

Ryu Dan believed he knew the answer. In all 317 betrayals, it had been the Gold-Masked Man hiding behind the Black Tooth Gang.

Gwak Hyeon’s lips trembled.

“The Gold-Masked Man is dead. He died last night.”

Ryu Dan’s fingers went still.

“This order came from a monk with seven eyes on his face. He said you would stop my signal before I sent it.”

An unknown patron.

An order that anticipated Ryu Dan’s preemption.

As Ryu Dan moved to crush the incense tube, Gwak Hyeon bit down on a left molar.

The signal was not the incense.

It was his blood.

He made no attempt to escape. He bit his tongue and spat onto the bronze lines in the floor.

*Hiss!*

All seven blazed red.

[Scheduled deviation preempted.]

[Executing alternate condition.]

The entire passage tilted.

“Everyone, grab the wall!” Nam Sogun shouted as he sealed Gwak Hyeon’s acupoints.

He was not too late. He saw the direction of collapse, shoved three disciples to the opposite side, drove his sword into a gap in the stone, and looped a rope around it. Half the party would have fallen if he had relaxed merely because Ryu Dan had caught the traitor.

The known exit vanished into a wall.

Instead, the floor split open ahead.

A white corridor appeared.

Ryu Dan had never seen it.

Not once in 9,431 loops.

There were no swords within it. No scabbards. No people. Yet a sprinkle of stone dust that fell across the entrance separated into eight clean pieces.

Nam Sogun tossed a strand of silk forward.

It became dozens of fragments in midair.

“An invisible sword formation,” he said.

“No.”

Ryu Dan studied the severed ends. Sword aura would have thrown them all in one direction. These pieces had fallen to opposite sides.

“A sword formation has a wielder somewhere. The corridor itself is the blade.”

Behind them, red light climbed the walls. The chamber would seal in half a gak—less than eight minutes. No door remained behind. Ahead waited a road of blades beyond Ryu Dan’s memory.

“Which side is safe?” someone asked.

Ryu Dan could not answer.

For the first time, thousands of remembered deaths pointed to no path at all.

Nam Sogun pushed his scabbard into the corridor. An unseen force drove it downward, then flung it left. He released it before it could break.

“The blades aren’t moving,” he said. “The force carries from one place to the next.”

He was right.

A living examiner was not baggage waiting for Ryu Dan to provide every answer.

Ryu Dan closed his eyes and listened.

*Hummm.*

Invisible pressure circulated between floor and walls. Once a force struck, it did not vanish. It passed into the next cutting line.

Like a serving tray.

When a bowl of soup began sliding across a tilted tray, trying to stop it only spilled the soup. Ryu Dan would turn with the tray, send the force around the rim, and use it to power his next step.

This corridor was merely faster and heavier.

He looked down at the Plum-Shadow weighted shoes. Their dark-iron plates could nail down his center. Their lateral steps could keep him from meeting an edge head-on.

He closed one hand around the Myriad-Poison Ward Bead. The detoxification method settled in his third star node did not stop poison at the skin. It guided the invading poison from point to point, then drove it out.

His feet could do the same.

Do not stop the force.

Turn it toward the next center.

“I’ll go inside,” Ryu Dan said.

Nam Sogun blocked him.

“You said you don’t know this road.”

“That’s why I have to go. If I knew it, I’d explain.”

“And if you fail?”

Ryu Dan glanced at the rising red lines.

“Hall Master, take everyone three steps back. The first pressure comes from above. The second comes from the right. That is as much as we have observed.”

He did not pretend to have every answer. He entrusted only what he knew.

Nam Sogun stepped aside.

“I’ll watch the inner door. You watch your feet.”

Ryu Dan entered the corridor.

The first pressure crashed onto the crown of his head.

He angled one weighted shoe and shed the force sideways. His wounded shoulder felt ready to split. The diverted impact struck the floor, then returned from the right wall.

Ryu Dan did not dodge.

As though circulating poison to the next point, he turned the impact from right foot to waist and waist to left foot. His body slid like a bowl across a tray.

Second step.

An unseen edge sliced his hem.

On the third, the weighted shoe followed half a beat too slowly. His center tipped backward. Every footwork art in his memories was wrong by one beat.

Ryu Dan stopped choosing from the past.

He remembered the inn.

When three guests shoved his tray from different sides at once, he did not resist the fall. He stepped half a pace in the direction of the spill and turned the tray with it.

His body leaned.

Ryu Dan sent a foot toward the fall rather than away from it. His left foot caught his center. The weight of the Plum-Shadow shoe circled through his heel. Rebounding pressure entered that circle and became power for his next step.

*Boom!*

His body spun half a turn between the invisible edges.

One step repaid the next.

Returning Dish Step.

A footwork art that returned borrowed force and purchased another pace.

[Unregistered footwork observed.]

[Combination detected: Plum-Shadow Steps, poison circulation, non-martial balance method.]

The formation had no name for it.

Ryu Dan did.

“Returning Dish Step.”

From the fourth step onward, the corridor’s pressure ceased to be an enemy. When it struck from above, he turned aside. When it cut from the side, he poured it into the floor. The Plum-Shadow shoes made an axis. The detoxification cycle mapped the path of impact. The balance learned from carrying thousands of trays chose the next center.

By the time he reached the far end, his sleeves hung in strips.

Only three shallow lines marked his skin.

He turned a handle in the wall.

*Thoom!*

Every invisible edge stopped.

The other eleven rushed through. Jin Hu grabbed Ryu Dan’s shoulder, saw the wounds, and snatched his hand away.

“You’re alive.”

“For now.”

Nam Sogun studied the round traces Ryu Dan’s feet had left on the floor. They belonged neither to Mae Hwajin nor to the Azure Peak Sect.

“That step. Who taught you?”

“The guests at the inn.”

“What sect are they?”

“The Hungry Sect. They get angry when I spill their soup.”

This time, Nam Sogun laughed too.

The door at the end of the corridor opened, and light poured out.

They had reached the geniuses’ secret vault.

Weapons, medicine cases, manuals, and formation stones filled the walls in tier after tier: Baek Mugyeol’s wooden sword, Tang Soryeong’s poison case, a lightness-training sash left by Mae Hwajin, Gwi Sam’s black-iron tablet. The seven regressors had hidden these things in the outer tomb so none of the others could take them.

The disciples forgot to breathe.

Ryu Dan knew where almost everything was. If he claimed it all, no one could have stopped him. He had opened the way, and he understood the objects better than anyone present.

He took only Gwi Sam’s black-iron tablet, which could distinguish formation fluids marked with the seven-star pattern.

Then he tossed the lightness-training sash to Jin Hu.

“You’re fast, but your knees spread when you land. Wear that and they’ll correct themselves.”

Jin Hu caught it on reflex.

Ryu Dan turned to the disciple who had reached for the life door.

“The Clear-Sight Lens. You judge energy by the glow on its surface. Use this no more than one double-hour a day.”

A heart-guarding mirror went to Jin Hu’s companion, whom Ryu Dan had saved from the poison-needle pressure plate. A coil of Heavenly Silkworm Thread went to the disciple who had roped the rear line to safety.

Before Nam Sogun, Ryu Dan placed Baek Mugyeol’s wooden sword.

“You’re giving me one too?”

“You’ll use it better than I would.”

“The merit of finding this vault is yours.”

“If I carry everything out alone, I’ll have to enter the next place alone too.”

Ryu Dan had not divided treasure.

He had acquired people who would help open the next door.

Jin Hu fastened the training sash and offered a formal salute.

“I’ll be the first to ask that they remove *provisional* from your rank.”

The others bowed in turn. Until moments ago, they had stood behind Ryu Dan to avoid traps. Now they stood with him because they had seen what he chose to call his share.

Nam Sogun tested Baek Mugyeol’s wooden sword. The tremor at its tip vanished.

“Don’t collect the price in lives,” he said. “I’ll repay you as a rival.”

“That sounds more expensive.”

“Then you chose well.”

They did not abandon Gwak Hyeon. Nam Sogun tied him to a formation stone at the vault entrance and checked his breathing. The art that had turned his blood into a signal had rooted itself into the pathways around his heart.

“We punish the betrayal,” Nam Sogun said, “but first we find the man who tried to silence the traitor.”

He ordered two disciples to bring Gwak Hyeon out alive. In every loop Ryu Dan remembered, the traitor had died here. This time, both his crime and the hand behind it would return to the mountain gate.

The rescue had preserved more than lives. Wearing the training sash, Jin Hu took command of the rear line. The disciple with the Clear-Sight Lens discovered a vent behind a false wall. The treasures Ryu Dan had distributed immediately became the party’s eyes and feet.

Ryu Dan went to the back of the vault.

A black stone case waited there, its lid engraved with breathing lines like tongues of flame.

Yeom Gaha.

The demonic heir had guarded the sealing breath within until the moment of death in every loop. Force the case open, and the demonic energy inside would burn the intruder’s heart. It needed no key.

It needed an opposite breath.

Ryu Dan formed a seal with his right hand between nose and mouth.

A short inhale. Two breaks. Then an exhale longer than the breath he had taken in.

The pattern Yeom Gaha had used whenever sealing his rampaging demonic energy.

At the first breath, the red lines on the case dimmed.

At the second, demonic energy climbed Ryu Dan’s arm. The Myriad-Poison Ward Bead remained silent. This was not poison. It was power the bead could not stop.

Ryu Dan applied the Returning Dish Step within his own body.

He did not oppose the invading force. He circulated it around his third star node and turned it toward the fourth star, still sealed.

Yeom Gaha’s breathing pattern closed the final knot.

*Click.*

The stone case opened.

Black flame rushed into Ryu Dan’s dantian. His bones heated from within. His breath caught in his throat.

He did not reach for more.

He accepted only as much as his present body could endure, then used the sealing breath to return the remainder to the case and shut it away.

The fourth star around his dantian ignited.

[Fourth star node of the Meridian of Ten Thousand Rehearsals opened.]

[Yeom Gaha’s demonic sealing breath established.]

Ryu Dan opened his palm. A thread of black energy rose above it. It did not rage outward. It circled once between his fingers and stopped exactly where he willed.

He had not increased his power.

He had opened a door through which violent power could be turned and sealed.

“Ryu Dan,” Jin Hu called from the other side of the vault. “This place is empty.”

Seven stone pedestals stood in a circle. Six bore marks where objects had once rested. The dust on the final pedestal had been wiped clean.

Characters were engraved at its base.

*Seol Muyeon.*

*Outer Gate Key.*

Ryu Dan knew that pedestal.

The key opened the outermost gate of the Tomb of Seven Returns, a gate none of the regressors had ever breached. It was the seventh foundation and the only route toward the heart of the formation.

But it was gone.

Nam Sogun examined the marks around it.

“Someone took it before we arrived. The dust settled to different heights. It wasn’t long ago.”

Ryu Dan looked at the floor.

No footprints.

Instead, seven black dots marked the stone behind the pedestal. Formation script slowly surfaced beneath them.

[Outer Gate Key removed.]

[Bearer record: none.]

Outside the vault, Nam Sogun counted the expedition again under his own direction. Twelve had entered, and all twelve were present. Even Gwak Hyeon was still breathing, bound to a stretcher. The promise Ryu Dan had revised at the entrance had been kept.

Across 9,431 loops, even the seven regressors had failed to open this entire vault.

Yet something had crossed a road beyond Ryu Dan’s memory, arrived before him, and taken only the Outer Gate Key.

Ryu Dan rested a hand on the empty pedestal and felt the heat of his fourth star.

Today, he had crossed a corridor of blades he had never seen.

Next, he would have to find the one who had walked ahead of him.
