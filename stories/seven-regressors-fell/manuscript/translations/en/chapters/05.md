# Chapter 5. A World Without Regressors

The first thing Ryu Dan checked when Return Inn came into view was the roof.

It was intact.

No shattered tiles. No arrows buried in the eaves. While he had been away exploring the outer reaches of the Tomb of Seven Returns, no fresh disaster had struck. Beside the crockery jars, Ma Yeongsun was spreading flour sacks out to dry.

Alive.

Only after seeing that with his own eyes did Ryu Dan breathe out.

“Dan.”

Ma Yeongsun beckoned him over. “You have rather a lot of guests.”

Blue uniforms blocked the road in front of the inn. Azure Peak Sect martial artists. Beggars in patched clothes occupied the alley to the right, while members of the Poison Sect stood beneath the teahouse awning opposite, black medicine cases at their waists.

Three factions had surrounded one shabby inn.

Nam Sogun, who had returned with Ryu Dan, frowned. “Rumor traveled too quickly.”

Eleven people had seen what happened in the tomb. The three shallow cuts the blade corridor had left beneath Ryu Dan’s sleeve had only just begun to sting.

Eleven mouths traveled faster than horses on the open road.

Some claimed Ryu Dan was the thief who had murdered Baek Mugyeol. Others called him Baek’s secret disciple. One man had apparently insisted that Ryu Dan was Baek Mugyeol rejuvenated.

Rather than sort truth from nonsense, Ma Yeongsun set a table across the entrance.

“Two taels to enter.”

An Azure Peak disciple stared. “Do you have any idea who we are?”

“I do. That’s why it’s two. Guests without swords only pay one.”

The beggars roared with laughter. Ma Yeongsun put out another sign.

**Beggars’ standing room: one tael.**
**Roof and wall premium seats: three taels.**

The laughter stopped.

A young Poison Sect apothecary named So Yagran spoke coldly. “We did not come to watch a show.”

“Then you came to eat. Rice soup is double.”

“Why?”

“Bowls used by poisoners have to be boiled twice.”

No one found an answer to that.

In the end, all three factions paid.

Ma Yeongsun seated Azure Peak along the second-floor railing, the Beggars’ Union beside the first-floor windows, and the Poison Sect at the table farthest from the kitchen. If anyone drew a blade, the backs of the other two factions would be directly in front of them.

Ryu Dan studied the arrangement with respect. Nine thousand iterations of formation lore had taught him less about controlling a room than one innkeeper with a seating chart.

The Poison Sect made the first move.

So Yagran pushed a blue teacup across the table. “Our sect master prepared this Heart-Clearing Antidote Tea. Drink it and remain unharmed, and we will withdraw our suspicion that you stole the Myriad-Poison Ward Bead.”

“And if I collapse?”

“Then you answer for using a false treasure to swindle people.”

The tea smelled faintly of plum blossoms. Its color was clear. To everyone watching, it looked like a rare medicinal brew.

Ryu Dan did not pick it up. He turned the saucer with one finger.

“You didn’t make this tea, Miss So.”

Her eyes narrowed. “What makes you say that?”

“Poison Sect apothecaries warm the cup before pouring. The walls are hot, but the foot is cold.”

He tilted the cup. White powder settled along one side of the bottom.

“And Heart-Clearing Antidote Tea leaves no sediment.”

He held the Myriad-Poison Ward Bead above the tea. The black mist inside the bead did not stir.

It was not poison.

The corner of So Yagran’s mouth lifted. “Even your bead agrees.”

“Because this is counterfeit antidote.”

Ryu Dan poured the tea into a pot containing a withered orchid. A yellowed leaf briefly regained its green color—then the stem snapped with a dry click.

The medicine forced one’s energy to surge, then hardened the meridians. A drinker would feel purified for a few moments and lose the ability to circulate qi half a day later.

“This wasn’t meant to make me fall here,” Ryu Dan said. “You wanted witnesses to see me drink it safely. Then, once we entered the tomb, my internal energy would suddenly fail.”

So Yagran slowly turned her head.

The elderly servant who had delivered the tea had curled his thumb into his palm: a discarded Poison Sect hand sign once used to conceal a lie.

The servant lunged.

Ryu Dan flicked the saucer. Its rim struck the servant’s ankle bone and curved back.

Crack!

He fell face-first across the threshold, three packets of identical white powder spilling from his sleeve.

So Yagran tasted a speck. Her expression froze.

“This did not come from our dispensary.”

“I know. The knots are Black Tooth Gang knots.”

In an instant, the Poison Sect’s suspicion shifted from Ryu Dan to the Black Tooth Gang.

So Yagran retrieved her cup, then offered Ryu Dan a formal salute. “I will repay the debt for exposing the counterfeit.”

“Repay it now. Someone will need an antidote shortly.”

She asked no questions. Her medicine case landed on the table.

An enemy’s hostility had become a useful preparation.

The Beggars’ Union came next.

A gaunt old beggar by the window tapped the floor with his bamboo staff.

Tap. Tap-tap. Pause. Tap.

The inn went quiet. It was a Beggars’ Union dispatch cipher.

*Who opened Gwi Sam’s hidden vault?*

Ryu Dan set down three empty soup bowls in order: large, small, upside down. Then he laid one chopstick diagonally between them.

*The man who opened it is dead. The debt he left behind lives.*

The old beggar’s eyes flashed. His staff sounded again.

*Whose silver is it?*

Ryu Dan produced the black iron token he had recovered, along with the thin strip of silk hidden behind it. Tiny names and numbers appeared when sunlight touched the cloth.

It was a ledger of people who had lent silver to Gwi Sam and never been repaid.

Ryu Dan slid it forward.

*Not the property of the man who ate. Return it to those who starved.*

The old beggar stared at the silk. Beside him, a younger beggar named U Chil could not close his mouth. Knowing Gwi Sam’s final cipher was astonishing enough. Returning a creditor ledger worth far more than the hidden silver, without conditions, was something else entirely.

The old beggar rose.

“The Qingliu Branch of the Beggars’ Union will record Young Hero Ryu as the man who settled Gwi Sam’s debts.”

It was only one sentence, but from this day forward the local beggars would bring Ryu Dan news before they sold news about him.

U Chil collected the empty bowls. “Since you returned the cipher, I’ll clear these.”

Ma Yeongsun hurried over and stacked another bowl on top. “Since you’re already up.”

The negotiation with the Beggars’ Union ended with the inn acquiring a new dishwasher.

Azure Peak was last.

A young swordsman leaped down from the second floor. He had not joined Nam Sogun’s tomb expedition and had never seen Ryu Dan in action.

“Word has spread that our Outer Hall Master was deceived by a common waiter. For Azure Peak’s honor, I will verify the truth myself.”

His hand went to his sword.

Ryu Dan left both hands at his sides. “You should not draw that.”

“Afraid?”

“The ornament on your hilt is caught inside the mouth of your scabbard.”

The swordsman scoffed and pulled.

The blade came out half an inch, then stopped. A decorative cord replaced in haste the previous night had curled inward. Red-faced, he swung the sheathed weapon instead.

Ryu Dan did not dodge aside. With the Returning Dish Step, he slipped inside the force already tipping forward. His shoulder propped up the swordsman’s elbow. One finger pressed the end of the scabbard.

The swordsman’s own strength rolled around his waist and dropped into his knees.

Thud.

He knelt without ever drawing his sword.

Ryu Dan freed the tangled cord. “Azure Peak’s sword is weakest before it leaves the scabbard. A man in a hurry pulls the hilt first. Push the guard with your thumb, then draw.”

The swordsman rose through clenched teeth. This time he pushed the guard.

Shing.

The blade came free smoothly, faster than before, without breaking his stance.

“Once you lose, learn,” Nam Sogun said from above.

The young man sheathed his sword, cheeks burning. “...I accept the lesson.”

Ryu Dan had ended Azure Peak’s challenge without drawing a blade. The laughter was brief; the consequences were not. The Poison Sect had supplied medicines, the Beggars’ Union had promised information, and Azure Peak converted Ryu Dan’s provisional placement into official first rank in the Outer Hall.

A bell rang outside.

Ting.

The spectators parted.

A broker with his face wrapped in white cloth entered carrying a black box stamped with seven stars.

“The outer-gate key stolen from the Tomb of Seven Returns will be auctioned here.”

Nam Sogun’s hand went to his sword. So Yagran shut her medicine case. The old beggar planted his staff.

Ryu Dan watched the broker’s shoes.

There was no mud on them.

This man had not descended from the mountain. Someone had brought him to the inn from nearby.

The auction had never happened during the loops. Ryu Dan did not know who would buy the key, whether the box was trapped, or which faction the broker served.

So he stopped trying to read the object and began reading the people.

Ma Yeongsun seized the table in front of the broker. “Ten taels for use of the premises. Ten percent of the winning bid.”

“I came to hold an auction.”

“Which means you need a table.”

After a silence, the broker paid.

Ma Yeongsun flipped the upstairs sign.

**Auction premium seats: five taels.**
**Unlimited tea: ten taels.**
**When the leaves run out, water is served.**

The battlefield became a marketplace.

The broker opened his box. A piece of white jade two finger-joints long lay inside. Beneath a door-shaped groove was carved the character for *outer*.

The black iron token inside Ryu Dan’s robe grew hot.

The key was genuine.

“Bidding begins at three hundred taels. Martial techniques and information will also be valued.”

The Poison Sect offered four hundred. Azure Peak called five. The Beggars’ Union offered knowledge of three secret passages around the tomb.

The price climbed.

Ryu Dan remained silent and watched hands.

A silk-robed merchant in the corner called one thousand while pressing his left sleeve with his right hand. It looked like the motion of a man checking banknotes, but his thumb pointed outward rather than in. It was the habit of someone pretending to have a purse that was not there.

“Twelve hundred!” the merchant called.

“Can you pay now?” Ryu Dan asked.

“You dare question my wealth?”

“No. I question the person inside your left sleeve.”

Nam Sogun pinned the sleeve with his scabbard. A paper doll fell out, red words painted across its chest.

**Raise the price.**

A puppet talisman. A false bidder.

So Yagran pinned it to the table with a needle while U Chil blocked the merchant’s escape.

The broker’s eyes wavered for the first time.

Ryu Dan caught it. The broker had not known the false bidder’s identity either. Someone else was manipulating the auction.

“That bid is void,” Ryu Dan said.

“Are you the auctioneer?”

“No. But if you want an honest price, remove the dishonest one first.”

He looked at the three factions.

“Combine your bids.”

An Azure Peak swordsman frowned. “Under whose name?”

“Mine.”

“That is your idea of combining?”

“In exchange, I will pay each sect something it needs now.”

He turned to So Yagran first. “The scent of the Poison Sect’s Soul-Chasing Powder changes on the third breath. Covering the nose does nothing; the collarbone stiffens first. Don’t eliminate the scent. Reverse the first and second fragrances, and no one will detect the change.”

Her eyes widened. It was a flaw only three elders in her sect were studying.

Next came the old beggar. “Gwi Sam’s cipher exposes the messenger because everyone pauses differently. Stop counting the pause. Count how high the staff leaves the floor. Then the dispatch remains identical no matter who carries it.”

The old beggar tested it. Tap. Tap-tap. The meaning was unchanged, but his personal rhythm had vanished.

Last, Ryu Dan faced Nam Sogun. “Correcting the final movement of the Thirteen Falling Clouds left the eighth transition open. After the seventh form, don’t lower the point. Raise your wrist and flow into the ninth. You keep the force from the correction.”

Nam Sogun linked the two movements in the air. His sword stayed sheathed, but all thirteen candles along the railing flickered at once.

Three expressions changed.

Ryu Dan was not selling the future. He was converting defects observed over nine thousand loops into a price paid by people he could trust now.

“Azure Peak guarantees five hundred taels,” Nam Sogun said.

So Yagran put down her medicine case. “The Poison Sect adds two Great Restoration Antidote Pills. Market value: four hundred.”

The old beggar tucked away the silk ledger. “The Beggars’ Union guarantees an honest market for any treasure recovered from the tomb—and stakes its name on the truth of this bid.”

Ryu Dan added twenty taels of his own.

Ma Yeongsun clicked her tongue. “So good at bundling other people’s money, yet you spend your own too.”

“It’s my first time buying tomorrow.”

“Tomorrow charges a fine price.”

The broker weighed the offers. They totaled less than one thousand, but every part could actually be collected. More importantly, no one in Qingliu County could cheat a buyer backed by all three factions and expect to live comfortably afterward.

“Sold.”

The white-jade key dropped into Ryu Dan’s palm.

He had expected it to be cold.

It burned.

[Outer-gate key of the Tomb of Seven Returns bound.]

[New owner of the outer-gate formation: Ryu Dan.]

His first, second, third, and fourth star nodes rang in turn.

He had won an auction absent from every memory—not by recalling a hiding place or knowing tomorrow’s price, but by reading a liar’s hand, a broker’s hesitation, and the trust he had earned that very day.

The crowd saw the difference. Those who had treated Ryu Dan as a rumor attached to Baek Mugyeol’s name opened a path for him. Nam Sogun fastened the Outer Hall badge at his waist. So Yagran engraved Ryu Dan’s name inside the Poison Sect medicine case. The old beggar tied a small knot around an inn pillar, marking Return Inn as a place where information could be bought but not stolen from its owner.

One waiter’s victory changed the inn’s rules.

Ma Yeongsun immediately posted a sign.

**Tomb inquiries and appointments with Young Hero Ryu, tea included: twenty taels.**

“That is an appointment with me,” Ryu Dan said.

“You get a share. Two exemptions from washing dishes.”

Whether that was worth more than silver required thought.

The broker’s body suddenly folded.

Black sand poured from beneath his collar. He had never been human. As the empty shell collapsed, a seven-star design spread across the inn floor.

Ting.

A cracked bronze bell rang by itself.

[Outer gate opening detected.]

[Replaying preserved optimal record.]

[Gate-recording spirit Myogak awakening.]

A shadow rose from the floor: a gaunt monk in gray robes, his face marked by seven black points in place of eyes.

Ryu Dan knew him.

In every loop, that figure had watched the seven regressors’ choices from the deepest chamber of the tomb. They had mistaken it for the formation’s shadow.

Now the shadow looked directly at him.

“Recording vessel.”

An enemy remembered him.

Myogak raised one hand.

Ryu Dan lifted the outer-gate key at once. “Innkeeper, get back!”

Ma Yeongsun ducked while clutching the cashbox. Myogak’s hand swept through the space she had occupied.

It seemed to miss.

Then a red seal spread across her apron.

**RETURN.**

It was the mark that had drawn her death toward the formation’s center in every cycle.

“I reclaim the hand that moved the records.”

The seal inverted.

Space folded like a closing door and swallowed Ma Yeongsun. Ryu Dan caught the apron tie, but the knot came loose, leaving only a strip of cloth in his grip.

Her voice flew through the narrowing gap.

“Tell him he dies if he touches the cashbox!”

The opening shrank to the width of a finger.

If Ryu Dan waited until he was fully prepared, he would be too late.

He drove the outer-gate key into the gap.

Boom!

The closing space tore open again. Cold air from the Tomb of Seven Returns flooded the inn.

Ryu Dan looked at his three new allies. “Hall Master Nam, sever the star on the left. Miss So, prepare the antidote incense. U Chil, mark our route back.”

He had no right to command them.

They moved anyway.

Nam Sogun’s sword split a red star. So Yagran’s incense poured into the black opening. U Chil’s staff scratched the first return marker into the floor.

Ryu Dan threw himself through the door.

An unknown enemy.

An unknown road.

And one person he would bring home no matter what.

This was no pursuit guided by memory.

For the first time, Ryu Dan chased tomorrow on his own two feet.
