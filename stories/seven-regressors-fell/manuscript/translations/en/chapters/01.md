# Chapter 1. Today Did Not Return

Bong—

The old bell at Return Inn announced the dawn.

Ryu Dan opened his eyes.

Bong—

The second toll ran beneath the eaves and rattled the guest-room doors.

Bong—

The third.

He could not make himself rise.

The bell rang every morning. It had rung on the first day, the thirteenth, and the thirtieth. It had never rung on the dawn of day thirty-one. Whenever any one of the seven marked by the Seven-Star Return Formation died, the world went back to the first day.

That had happened 9,431 times.

Yet today, the bell had rung.

“Thirty-one.”

The number felt strange in his mouth.

Ryu Dan opened the door. A roof tile that should have cracked last night was whole. Sunlight hung at an angle he had never seen at the end of the gallery. Boiled eggs, wet earth, Ma Yeongsun’s ladle striking the kitchen cauldron—everything familiar had been gathered inside one unfamiliar day.

The seven regressors had not returned.

On the thirtieth day of the final loop, all seven had died inside the Tomb of Seven Returns. Ryu Dan had seen Baek Mugyeol’s broken sword, Tang Soryeong’s charred hands, and the last techniques they had thrown out trying to save one another.

They were not the only ones who had lost their lives.

Ryu Dan had lost 9,431 lives as well. His body returned to the first day, but the bite of a blade, the stink of burning flesh, and the first step each person took never left him. The formation chose the nearest unmarked human as its recording vessel. By unfortunate chance, that was the inn boy who had spent the longest time in the inn’s underground rooms.

No one else knew.

“I’m alive.” A low laugh escaped him.

“Who is?”

Ma Yeongsun looked up from the foot of the stairs. At forty-three she had more gray in her hair than she should have, and eyes as sharp as when she found a false figure in her ledger.

“Me.”

“You slept all night. Being alive is the least you can do. Two buckets of water for the privilege.”

“You’re alive too.”

“Then mine is three.”

She remembered none of it: how she had died in almost every loop, or how many times Ryu Dan had held her body until the world returned.

That was better.

He smiled and rolled up his sleeves.

The inn door slammed open.

“Old Ma! We’re here for the interest!”

A Black Tooth Gang thug shouldered inside, a scar running down his cheek. Two underlings followed.

Ryu Dan had seen him 418 times.

On each of those mornings, the man kicked the same table, made the same threats, and broke the same bowl. The loops changed. A low-ranking Black Tooth thug’s imagination did not.

“If you don’t have the money, hand over the—”

“Inn. If you won’t hand over the inn, you’ll take my wrist. If neither suits us, you’ll break the owner’s knees.” Ryu Dan set down his buckets. “Then you’ll say, ‘Not even the magistrate’s office can cheat the Black Tooth Gang.’ You’ll kick the table on your right and draw your saber half an inch.”

The thug’s mouth stayed open.

“Who told you?”

“You just did.”

A guest eating porridge in the corner smothered a laugh. The thug’s neck reddened.

“You little bastard.”

He kicked the table on his right.

As expected.

A white porcelain bowl flew from the tilting edge. It had broken every time. In the first loop, Ryu Dan had not known it was part of Ma Yeongsun’s wedding set. Around the hundredth, he tore the back of his hand trying to catch it. By the thousandth he knew its angle. By the nine-thousandth he could place it from the sound alone.

He extended his serving tray.

The bowl spun half a turn. Its force flowed around the raised rim, and it came to rest at the center without spilling a drop.

“Oh.” The porridge guest did not hide his admiration this time.

Ma Yeongsun snatched up the bowl first. “You earned the price of one bowl.”

“Could you praise the person before the crockery?”

“The person didn’t break.”

The thug gripped his saber.

Half an inch of steel appeared.

Ryu Dan had no inner power. His dantian was empty and his meridians blocked. Seeing Baek Mugyeol’s sword 2,117 times did not give him the strength to use it. He had memorized Mae Hwajin’s qinggong, but in this body, attempting it would only make him trip over his own feet.

But he had seen this hand draw this saber 418 times.

Ryu Dan pressed the edge of the tray against the end of the scabbard. He followed the thug’s pull for half a step, then turned the tray upright. The blade stayed trapped while the scabbard twisted. The instant the man’s elbow folded against his ribs, Ryu Dan tapped the flat of the tray.

“Ghk!”

The thug dropped to one knee, wrist bent. His undrawn saber hit the floor.

Ryu Dan covered it with the tray. “I forgot the part where drawing your saber twists your wrist.”

The two underlings exchanged looks. One made a fist, then quietly moved his right foot back when he noticed Ryu Dan watching it.

“What?” Ryu Dan asked.

“Nothing.”

“You looked ready to kick me.”

“My leg went numb.”

“You’ll live a long life.”

Laughter burst through the inn.

Ryu Dan released the thug, who tucked his throbbing wrist against his chest. “I’ll let this pass today. We’ll see if that mouth is still attached when I come back.”

“You won’t be back.”

“What?”

“Your gang leader is about to summon you to find the man who stole his ledger. It’s the one on your left.”

The left underling went white. The thug turned his head very slowly.

“How do you—”

“You’ll take a worse beating if you’re late.”

The three shoved and tangled their way out. Cries of “Was it really you?” and “No, Brother!” receded down the road.

Ma Yeongsun pushed the tray into Ryu Dan’s arms. “You can be miraculous later. Water.”

“Aren’t you surprised?”

“Unless your miracle will pay our debt, water.”

He picked up the buckets. He had heard that line thousands of times among the 9,431 loops. Today, however, she added something new.

“And stay in the kitchen for a while.”

His feet stopped. “Why?”

“You mocked three madmen before breakfast. They may return for revenge.”

Ma Yeongsun remembered no returns, yet danger still made her think of hiding him first.

Ryu Dan went to the rear yard without answering.

He was not the one who would soon be in danger.

Day thirty-one was new. The assassin’s habits were not. Soon after the thug’s 418-times-repeated threat, an assassin always crept onto the inn roof. Sometimes the Black Tooth Gang had sent him. Sometimes it was someone hunting the entrance to the tomb. His employer changed from loop to loop.

The result did not.

Ma Yeongsun died while carrying wine to a guest, a throwing knife in her throat.

The first time, Ryu Dan only screamed. The eighty-seventh, he blocked the door. The three-hundredth, he locked her in a room; the assassin came through the roof. The thousandth, he warned everyone, but no one believed an inn boy. In later loops, the regressors killed the assassin, yet they rarely managed to keep Ma Yeongsun alive through the fighting.

Today the seven were gone.

So Ryu Dan would do it.

He emptied the kitchen cabinets and moved Ma Yeongsun’s knives and ladles into the family quarters. He suspended a sack of flour from a pulley on the ceiling beam, replaced the old rope, and tied the knot so one pull would split the sack’s bottom.

At the woodshed door, he drew both hinge pins halfway out. He stacked three bundles of firewood in a loose circle where the door would fall.

Ma Yeongsun watched with her hands clasped behind her back.

“I told you to stay in the kitchen. You appear to be dismantling it.”

“Take today off.”

“And the business?”

“We’ll do twice as much tomorrow.”

“Will the guests eat twice as much?”

“Charge them twice as much.”

Her eyes flickered. “An excellent plan.”

She went inside without another question. Before closing the door, she said, “Don’t die. If I hire a new boy, I’ll have to feed him twice before he learns anything.”

The sun climbed over the eaves.

Ryu Dan set a single bowl in the middle of the kitchen and waited.

A tile scraped, very softly.

Once.

Twice.

Seven steps from the southern eave. Three beams crossed. Then pressure from the right foot.

The same sequence that had entered his ears 9,431 times.

Click.

Ryu Dan pulled the rope.

The ceiling hatch opened. A sackful of flour crashed over the black figure dropping through the roof.

“What—!”

Blinded, the assassin flung a knife. Ryu Dan was already crouching. Instead of breaking the waiting bowl, the knife buried itself in an empty cabinet door.

Ryu Dan ran for the woodshed. The assassin followed his steps, deciding it would be quicker to kill the target than wipe his eyes. A habit revealed in loop 623.

“Stop!”

“No.”

Ryu Dan rolled aside at the threshold. The assassin kicked the woodshed door.

The loosened hinge pins flew free.

The heavy door toppled inward with him. The firewood beneath rolled in every direction. With nowhere firm to plant a foot, the assassin’s knee buckled.

Ryu Dan hauled on the flour-sack rope. It ran back through the pulley, wrapped the fallen door, and tightened. The assassin lay facedown beneath it, arms and waist bound.

He was several times stronger than Ryu Dan.

He was also in no position to use that strength.

When he gathered inner power, Ryu Dan pressed a stick behind his armpit. It blocked the precise point where qi was collecting—a point Tang Soryeong had discovered while failing to take this assassin alive in loop 1,904.

“Who are you?” the assassin demanded.

“An inn boy.”

“No inn boy knows that point!”

“You meet all kinds when you serve enough guests.”

Ryu Dan searched his sleeves and belt. Six poison needles. Three throwing knives. One black vial. Inside his robe, a folded scrap of map.

It showed a stable above nine stones, with the last marked red.

Ryu Dan knew where the assassin had come from and what he had collected first. He had simply never had the leisure to search this corpse.

“Why do you have this?”

The assassin stared at the map. “Give it back.”

“Will you answer if I ask what it’s worth?”

“I’ll kill you.”

“Then don’t answer. I already know where it points.”

The man’s eyes narrowed. His jaw bulged.

Ryu Dan raised a piece of firewood before his mouth.

Thunk.

A hair-thin needle shot from beneath the assassin’s tongue and sank into the wood. For the first time, his face stiffened. Even bound, he had believed that final measure remained his.

“How did you know that too?”

“It hit me last time.”

The assassin could not understand. There was no scar beneath Ryu Dan’s left jaw. Every wound vanished when the world returned to the first day.

The memory of poison piercing flesh and freezing his tongue had remained. Today, that memory became one small piece of wood.

The assassin strained. The door rose; the pulley rope creaked. With all his inner power, he could snap it.

Ryu Dan stepped back and kicked a wedge from under the pile. More logs rolled. One side of the door dropped, folding the assassin’s arm beneath his body. His shoulder twisted and his gathered qi spilled uselessly away.

“The harder you pull, the tighter the door becomes.”

It was a lie. The rope would break after perhaps three more attempts. But a man whose tongue needle had been predicted believed him.

The strength drained out of the assassin. “What do you want?”

“For no one to die today.”

“That’s all?”

“I’m also a little curious about your bounty.”

Ryu Dan rang the kitchen bell three times.

Ma Yeongsun emerged with a broom. Her gaze traveled over the bound assassin, the collapsed door, and the flour coating the kitchen.

“You’re alive.”

She checked Ryu Dan first.

“Yes.” His answer came a little late.

She poked the assassin with the broom. “Is he?”

“He is.”

“The magistrate pays bounties, doesn’t he?”

Ryu Dan laughed. It came before the tears.

Sunlight lay across the center of the yard. At this hour, Ma Yeongsun stood on both feet. No knife in her throat. No blood soaking her sleeve.

For the first time in 9,431 loops.

“Why are you laughing?”

“Business was good today.”

“One door, one sack of flour, and three bundles of firewood.”

“Take it out of the bounty.”

“Obviously. Out of your share.”

Even that sounded precious. It put his name into tomorrow’s accounts.

The constables arrived to find the assassin still pinned beneath the door. Their captain examined the markings on the needles and knives, and his expression changed.

“There’s a bounty on this killer. He murdered the head of an escort agency last month.”

The people gathered outside turned toward Ryu Dan. In the morning they had seen a serving boy with a clever tray. Now even the captain inspected the pulley knot and firewood arrangement again.

“You caught him alone?”

“The door and flour helped.”

Ma Yeongsun cut in at once. “Both are mine. Remember that when you divide the bounty.”

The crowd laughed as its tension broke. The captain gave her a stamped receipt for the prisoner and told Ryu Dan not to leave town. His voice no longer held the indulgence reserved for a child.

As the constables dragged the assassin away, the porridge guest peered through the ruined doorway. “Could I lodge here starting tomorrow? An inn that catches assassins sounds safer than the road.”

“Payment in advance,” Ma Yeongsun said.

The first person Ryu Dan saved had already brought them a customer.

After the constables left, he went to the stable and counted nine stones from the wall.

He dug into the soil beneath the ninth stone and uncovered a small wooden case wrapped in wax. When he lifted the lid, a clear, cool medicinal fragrance spread through the air.

A Meridian-Cleansing Pill.

It washed blocked meridians and planted the first seed of qi. In every loop someone else had reached it first. Baek Mugyeol swallowed it whole. Tang Soryeong cut it into four. Jang Cheoru ate it with the dirt and spent three days clutching his stomach.

Ryu Dan chose the safest method, from loop 7,202.

He dissolved the pill in warm water and drank it in three portions. The first became fire in his throat. The second battered at the obstructions in his chest and belly. With the third, a slender thread of qi sprouted beneath his dantian.

It hurt.

But it was not an unknown pain. He had watched it nine thousand times and counted the breaths beside hundreds of attempts. He removed the force from Baek Mugyeol’s breathing and took only what his present body could endure from Mae Hwajin’s posture.

Shoulders down.

Spine straight.

Weight divided across three points of each sole.

The scattering medicinal force gathered into one path. Heat circled his dantian. Then came the sensation of a star kindling in darkness.

Ryu Dan rose slowly.

This was not the body he had possessed that morning. He had not gained some ocean of inner power—only a palmful of true qi, newly opened meridians, and one fundamental stance corrected through nine thousand rehearsals.

That one thing was enough.

A rotten sliver of beam fell from the stable roof. Without looking up, Ryu Dan moved half a step. His shoulders and hips did not sway. When he caught the wood, its impact dispersed evenly through the three points beneath each foot.

For the first time, an answer known only to his mind belonged to his body.

Faint golden letters appeared before his eyes.

[First star node of the Meridian of Ten Thousand Rehearsals opened.]

Seven stars formed a ring around his dantian. One shone. Six remained sealed.

A final sentence engraved itself upon the evening air.

[Return 9,431 complete. Recording vessel Ryu Dan: alive.]

He stared at it for a long time.

Return complete.

Alive.

Not the vessel that had stored other people’s failures. The formation had written the name of the man who survived this unprecedented day.

Ma Yeongsun shouted from behind him.

“Ryu Dan! If you received the bounty, go buy me a door!”

He swept the golden words away with one hand.

“Yes, ma’am!”

The first unknown tomorrow could wait until the kitchen had a door.
