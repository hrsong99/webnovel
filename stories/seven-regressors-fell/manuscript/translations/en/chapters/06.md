# Chapter 6. A Tomorrow I Have Never Seen

There was no floor beyond the door.

Ryu Dan fell into darkness with Ma Yeongsun’s apron tie wrapped around his left hand. The outer-gate key in his right shone red.

“Step on three.”

Nam Sogun had jumped in after him. “Step on what?”

“Two. Three.”

A seven-star pattern appeared in empty air.

Four pairs of feet landed on its first star at once. Nam Sogun swallowed a curse, U Chil scratched their route back with the tip of his staff, and So Yagran lit her antidote incense before she had even found her balance.

The star caught them.

A moment later, they tumbled across the floor of a stone chamber in the Tomb of Seven Returns.

Red chains erupted before they could rise. One bound Nam Sogun’s sword wrist to a coffin, another chained So Yagran’s medicine case, and a third trapped U Chil’s staff. No chain came for Ryu Dan.

A vessel containing records did not need to be restrained.

U Chil struck his chain. Sparks flew. “What kind of tomb captures the people who came to help?”

“Break seven techniques, and the chains should release one at a time.”

“Is that a memory?”

Ryu Dan examined the red lines leading between the coffins. The structure had never existed before.

“No. I can see it now.”

Ma Yeongsun sat directly ahead, tied to a stone chair with the cashbox still locked in her arms.

“You’re late,” she said.

“Are you hurt?”

“Three plates broke.” She jerked her chin at Myogak. “That monk says he’ll take the washing-up fee in lives.”

Myogak stood in the chamber’s center. Seven coffins spread behind him like a fan, each bearing a dead regressor’s weapon.

Baek Mugyeol’s sword.

Tang Soryeong’s poison pill.

Mae Hwajin’s weighted shoe.

Gwi Sam’s twin daggers.

Yeom Gaha’s iron gauntlet.

Jang Cheoru’s spear.

Seol Muyeon’s ice blade.

Seven people’s most perfected attacks.

Ryu Dan had seen all of them across 9,431 loops.

The problem was that Myogak had watched even longer.

“Recording vessel Ryu Dan.”

All seven black points on Myogak’s face opened.

“I will restore the records within you to their proper location.”

“No.”

Ryu Dan ran.

The first coffin opened.

Baek Mugyeol’s Thirteen Falling Clouds.

No sword appeared, yet thirteen lines of sword light filled the chamber. Twelve sealed every route; the final line pierced the only place left to flee.

Ryu Dan did not flee.

“Nam Sogun. Third light.”

Nam Sogun’s blade drew a blue arc. He could not overpower the Thirteen Falling Clouds head-on, but moving the root of its third line by half an inch changed the spacing of all thirteen.

Clang!

Ryu Dan passed through the opening without losing a thread from his sleeve.

The displaced third line rebounded and severed the red chain on Nam Sogun’s wrist. Ryu Dan had not merely stopped the first attack. He had reclaimed his first ally.

Nam Sogun smiled despite himself. “Never seen a waiter use Azure Peak’s sword as a stepping-stone.”

“I’ll settle the price later.”

“I’ll charge dearly.”

Green smoke burst from the second coffin.

Tang Soryeong’s Formless Myriad-Poison Cloud.

Ryu Dan knew the old sequence: three steps back, hold his breath, draw cold air from the left wall.

So Yagran seized his shoulder. “Not that way. My incense changed the current.”

Memory would have carried him straight into the poison.

He dropped without hesitation. So Yagran kicked the incense burner, sending it sliding into the green cloud. The poisonous fog split down the middle.

Its two halves washed over the return seal binding So Yagran’s medicine case and dissolved it. The moment her arm came free, she scattered the counterfeit antidote powder from the inn over the mark. Its red glow vanished completely.

The second ally finished her own chain with her own knowledge.

“What does this one cost?” Ryu Dan asked.

“Two bundles of toxic herbs from the tomb.”

“Three.”

“Two and a half.”

They completed the bargain while escaping the cloud.

The third coffin shuddered.

Mae Hwajin’s Plum-Shadow Steps.

Invisible footprints stamped down on Ryu Dan’s shadow. If one landed, the weight of ten thousand pounds would lock his legs in place.

U Chil drew two iron hooks from the end of his staff and threw them—not at Ryu Dan, but at his shadow. A thin wire joining the hooks wrapped around an unseen ankle.

“Young Hero Ryu! That settles the price of my meal!”

“You still owe us one soup bowl.”

“I save your life and still have to wash dishes?”

“Then I’ll remember the debt.”

With a shout, U Chil hauled on the wire. The shadow twisted one inch. One inch was enough for Ryu Dan to escape Plum-Shadow Steps.

The invisible footprint landed on the corner of a coffin instead. The coffin tilted, slackening the red chain around U Chil’s staff. U Chil read the height of its movement, not the old cipher’s pause, and twisted.

Click.

The ring came free.

Three allies, three releases. Techniques that might only have been avoided if Ryu Dan stood alone were returning people to his side instead.

The fourth coffin opened.

Gwi Sam’s twin daggers flew at U Chil.

The young beggar paled. “Why are Senior Gwi’s lost blades trying to kill me?”

“He only perfected them after seven failures.”

Ryu Dan snapped Ma Yeongsun’s apron tie through the air. One dagger tangled in the cloth. He redirected it into the second blade.

Sparks burst as both buried themselves in the wall.

U Chil stared. A waiter had defeated a legendary Beggars’ Union technique with an apron string.

There was no time to admire it.

Yeom Gaha’s iron palms thundered from the fifth coffin.

Heavy force covered walls and floor alike. The attack had broken Ryu Dan’s bones more times than he cared to count.

“Everyone down!”

Only Ryu Dan remained standing.

He stepped past the center of the palm force with the Returning Dish Step. The fourth star node did not give him greater output; it closed and rerouted the impact already inside his body so it could not collect in one meridian. The force was slowest where it was strongest. He crossed both arms to divert the first strike, then used what remained to turn the second aside.

Boom!

He skidded back three steps, blood filling his mouth.

He had not blocked it perfectly.

But Yeom Gaha’s iron palm smashed the stone chair behind him.

Ma Yeongsun rolled across the floor, still tied to the pieces. So Yagran rushed in and cut her bonds.

“You took that on purpose to free me?” Ma Yeongsun asked.

“If the result is good, it was the plan.”

“Wipe your mouth. You’ll scare away the customers.”

She cleaned the blood from his lip with her sleeve.

The sixth coffin opened.

Jang Cheoru’s One-Point Heaven Spear.

The thrust folded space into a single point. There had always been one escape: pivot half a circle on the right foot and run along the shaft.

As Ryu Dan began the step, Nam Sogun’s scabbard struck his ankle.

His body tipped the opposite way. The spearpoint grazed his cheek.

“What was that for?”

“Look at the floor.”

The poison fog had eaten into the flagstones, lowering the right side by half an inch. Had Ryu Dan used the remembered footwork, his turn would have been late.

He used the tilt instead. His left hand pressed the spear shaft; one foot stepped on Nam Sogun’s scabbard and launched him upward.

Three running steps along the spear.

His heel struck Myogak’s jaw.

Crack!

For the first time, Myogak’s head snapped back.

One black point fell from his face.

[Gate-recording spirit damaged.]

[Deviation between optimal history and present: increasing.]

Ryu Dan understood as he landed.

Diverging memories were not merely a weakness. His allies were correcting memories that would have been wrong if he fought alone.

The seventh coffin opened.

Seol Muyeon’s Glacial White River.

Every trace of moisture in the chamber froze. Breath turned white and frost gathered on eyebrows. Ma Yeongsun picked up a broken chair leg and tapped the icy floor.

“Dan. Hollow here.”

Ryu Dan heard what she meant: a water channel beneath the stone.

The poison fog had eaten the flagstone thin, and Jang Cheoru’s spearpoint had scored a crack through it. Ryu Dan drove the dark-iron heel of Plum-Shadow Steps into that seam. The weakened stone could not bear the weight.

The stone broke. Groundwater surged up and swallowed the Glacial White River’s cold.

A wall of ice formed in an instant.

Ryu Dan shoved it forward.

Crash!

Myogak was sealed inside his own cold.

All seven attacks had ended.

Nam Sogun leveled his sword. U Chil pulled his wire taut. So Yagran bit open her final antidote pill. Ma Yeongsun took a ledger from the cashbox and wrung water from its corner.

“Finished?” she asked.

All six of Myogak’s remaining eyes lit at once inside the ice.

[Individual record replay failed.]

[Calling final version of seven-person combined assault.]

All seven coffins exploded.

Sword light carried poison fog. Plum-Shadow Steps pinned every retreat. Twin daggers stitched the shadows closed. Iron palms drove the center toward the spearpoint. Last came the ice blade, freezing every remaining possibility.

A perfect combined formation honed by seven regressors across nine thousand attempts.

Ryu Dan had seen it exactly once—on the final night of the 9,431st loop, when they had nearly killed Myogak.

He remembered the answer.

Two steps between the thirteen sword lights.

Wrap the poison fog in the left sleeve.

Deflect one dagger into the iron palm.

Then guide the spearpoint toward the ice blade.

His body executed the memory.

The first six connections were flawless. Sword light split; poison rolled backward. A dagger struck the iron palm, and spear and ice turned against one another.

The last connection came.

A blade of light grazed his left shoulder. In the remembered answer, he had retreated half a step. The seven forces would then return to Myogak and recombine.

Ryu Dan did not retreat.

He accepted a shallow cut.

Blood sprayed. The sword light caught on his shoulder and arrived half a breath late. That tiny delay sent the poison fog into the iron palm first. The palm drove the spear, and the spear shattered the center of the ice shield.

The common joint linking all seven techniques slipped out of alignment.

KRA-BOOM!

The perfect assault collapsed inward.

The chamber ceiling shook. Fragments of all seven coffins rained down as dust. Nam Sogun stared at Ryu Dan’s shoulder, jaw tight.

“You calculated the wound too?”

“Only the depth.”

It bled, but his arm still moved. He had used his own body to cut the knot in a perfected formation.

Myogak walked from the wreckage, one of his six remaining eyes cracked.

“Record replay confirmed.”

Cold slid down Ryu Dan’s back.

The combined assault had been both attack and question: *How far will the recording vessel follow memory?*

Myogak had added even Ryu Dan’s chosen shallow wound to the optimal history. Six eyes read his shoulder, knees, gaze, and breathing in turn.

Then Myogak did not repeat any prior movement.

His right hand formed a sword seal. His left shaped a poison art. His feet crossed opposite to Plum-Shadow Steps. He recombined the recorded beginnings of all seven techniques—and continued into none of their familiar forms. Every piece came from the past. Their order did not.

Ryu Dan had never seen them arranged into this move.

Thousands of remembered counters offered thousands of contradictory answers.

Myogak vanished.

Ryu Dan turned his neck against a sword and lowered his center against an iron palm. Myogak’s elbow rose through the gap between those two choices.

Whump!

Ryu Dan’s vision flashed white. A palm touched his ribs an instant later. The blow was not powerful, but in that moment it severed his breathing, meridian flow, and the foot that would have begun the Returning Dish Step.

He flew into the broken coffins.

For the first time, a wholly unfamiliar attack put him completely on the ground.

[Unregistered attack observed.]

[No response record.]

Myogak had learned that Ryu Dan could combine memories. So the guardian had rearranged recorded openings into a sequence no regressor had used. It looked new, but unlike Ryu Dan, Myogak had not created a single motion of its own.

“Records recombine.”

Myogak advanced.

Nam Sogun threw his sword. Myogak deflected it without looking. He caught So Yagran’s poison needles between his fingers. U Chil’s wire snapped before it touched him.

Every technique was read.

Ryu Dan tried to rise and dropped to one knee. Thousands of responses waited in his head. Pick one, and Myogak knew its opening. Combine two, and Myogak knew the joint where they met.

He could not win while searching the past for a faster answer.

Ma Yeongsun took a fistful of flour from her cashbox and flung it at Myogak’s face. It had been trapped in her sleeve while she carried sacks outside the inn.

Myogak merely turned his head. The flour scattered without touching him.

“I fed that boy and made him wash dishes nine thousand times,” she said, dusting her empty hands. “If those memories belong to anyone, I own a share.”

Her mouth hardened.

“You want to take my waiter’s entire lifetime and you’re stingier than a man arguing over broken plates.”

Myogak looked toward her.

In that instant, Ryu Dan stopped looking backward.

He saw now.

Nam Sogun’s right breath was shortened from throwing his sword.

So Yagran’s incense drifted toward the left side of the ceiling.

Every time U Chil’s broken wire tapped the floor, an eighth echo returned. A hollow had opened behind a wall that had not been hollow before.

The flour from Ma Yeongsun’s sleeve followed an invisible current that curved around Myogak’s left foot.

The flagstone tilted right where poison had dissolved it.

A thin film of water remained from the Glacial White River.

The missing black point beneath Myogak’s jaw distorted his measure of the left side.

None of these things had ever existed together in any loop.

And beneath Myogak’s feet, among the seven stars, lay an eighth empty place no one had ever stepped on.

Ryu Dan did not advance.

He did not retreat.

His right foot pressed the tilted stone and slid. His left came down on the curved face of a broken plate. The slide and the plate’s rotation carried his body at an angle belonging to no known technique.

One step and half a step.

Entering and leaving at once.

Myogak’s fingers pierced only his collar.

[Unregistered footwork.]

All six eyes shook.

Ryu Dan stood in the eighth place.

“First Tomorrow Step.”

The first step into a tomorrow that had never existed.

The moment he named it, the movement ceased to be an accident. It became his martial art.

Ryu Dan did not strike Myogak.

He held out a hand to Ma Yeongsun. “May I borrow a plate?”

“Broken ones are half price.”

A shard skimmed into his palm. He threw it at the bronze bell behind Myogak.

Clang!

A high, crooked note rang through the tomb, a pitch the bell had never made.

All six eyes turned toward the sound. It was in no record.

Ryu Dan entered Myogak’s reach in that single instant.

He clenched a fist, then opened it halfway.

The defense that read a punch closed inward.

Two extended fingers pierced the empty point beneath Myogak’s jaw.

Thk.

No grand ultimate art. No form inherited from a master.

One movement altered now, in answer to a wound that existed now.

The six black points remaining on Myogak’s face shattered.

[Prediction impossible.]

[No optimal record.]

Myogak collapsed into black sand.

Only a small Seven-Star Core remained in Ryu Dan’s hand. Seven layers of hair-fine script were engraved inside the core itself: the Final Return Record that Myogak had guarded beyond the outer gate, not a separate book. It preserved every loop’s deaths, choices, and return conditions through the very end.

Formation script filled the chamber.

[Ownership authority over the Seven-Star Return Formation acquired.]

[Choose.]

[Maintain return function?]

Ryu Dan did not hesitate.

“Dismantle it.”

The red lines linking the seven coffins broke. The force that had caged people inside the same days, reversed death, and turned one human being into background data went dark piece by piece.

For a moment, even the memories seemed to fade.

Ryu Dan closed his fist around the seven-star core.

“But you don’t get back what I learned.”

All four star nodes rang together.

The Meridian of Ten Thousand Rehearsals remained. So did the First Tomorrow Step. The prison made by the formation was gone, but the strength he had learned within it was his.

[Seven-Star Return Formation dismantled.]

[Recording authority, acquired history, and Final Return Record vested in owner.]

[Outer storehouses and passages preserved beneath Return Inn.]

[Surface gate bound to Return Inn.]

The central return chamber began to collapse. Beyond it, the tomb’s surviving outer storehouses and passages folded beneath Return Inn and bound themselves to the Outer Gate Key instead of falling.

“Exit!” Nam Sogun shouted.

Ryu Dan drove the outer-gate key into the floor. The door that opened did not lead to the mountain.

It opened in the middle of Return Inn.

Five people rolled through. Gold coins, medicinal herbs, and chests of weapons poured after them in a glittering waterfall—the unclaimed treasure stored in the tomb’s outer rooms. Last came a massive wooden signboard, which fell across the inn’s shattered wall and blocked the hole perfectly.

Ma Yeongsun sat atop a heap of gold.

She looked around once, then counted the broken dishes first.

“Three and a half.”

So Yagran blinked. “You acquired an entire tomb, and that is what matters first?”

“The tomb isn’t going anywhere. Dish money leaks if you stop counting.”

Nam Sogun laughed as he sheathed his sword. U Chil stared at a treasure chest larger than himself and did not touch it.

Everyone knew who owned it now.

Ryu Dan gave the outer-gate key to Ma Yeongsun. “Use this as the inn key.”

“It belongs to you.”

“The door opens into your inn.”

She studied him for a moment, then accepted it. “Fine. Pay your room charge on time.”

“No discount?”

“You broke plates today.”

The price of a victory no one had ever won was remarkably specific.

Night passed. The sun rose.

Ryu Dan stood beneath the eaves of Return Inn. He had watched the same dawn more than nine thousand times, but nothing overlapped with this light.

Day thirty-one had ended.

Even without the formation, time moved forward.

[Current date: Day 32.]

The brief message appeared, then faded.

Ryu Dan gazed at that number for a long time.

Then a thin bell sounded from behind the inn’s new signboard.

Ting.

It was not the voice of the dismantled formation.

Someone stood outside.

Beneath a dust-covered traveling hat was the unfamiliar face of a wounded woman. Blood soaked her flank, and a broken arrow shaft swung at her waist. Eight red rings—not seven—circled her wrist.

Her eyes swept the inn. Her expression hardened.

“Why did only seven return? There were supposed to be eight regressors.”
