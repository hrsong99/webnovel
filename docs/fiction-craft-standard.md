# Fiction craft standard

**Purpose:** a compact, genre-flexible standard for planning, drafting, reviewing, translating, and revising fiction. The research behind it lives in [`research/fiction-craft/fundamentals.md`](../research/fiction-craft/fundamentals.md).

This is not a formula and does not assign a literary score. A story may move through conflict, wonder, comedy, association, image, relationship, investigation, or pattern. It must nevertheless know what experience it intends and earn the reader’s continued attention.

## One-page checklist

Use these twelve questions first. Open the detailed sections only when one fails.

**Minimum viable use:** fill in the story contract before drafting; run this checklist after each chapter; reverse-outline the full draft before line editing; run the release gate after revision.

- [ ] **Experience:** Can we name what this story or chapter should make its intended reader feel?
- [ ] **Orientation:** Does a person, group, relationship, or consciousness seek, protect, avoid, investigate, endure, or refuse something now?
- [ ] **Pressure:** Is there resistance, uncertainty, obligation, or cost?
- [ ] **Agency:** Does a principal character’s action, speech, withholding, care, witness, error, or refusal affect an outcome or its meaning within credible constraints?
- [ ] **Change:** Is an option, understanding, relationship, commitment, emotional field, or pattern meaningfully different at the end?
- [ ] **Significance:** Is the importance felt through a specific body, object, relationship, lost future, or lasting emotional residue?
- [ ] **Page value:** Does each stretch add pressure, knowledge, choice, consequence, relationship movement, or pleasure?
- [ ] **Information:** Does exposition arrive because someone needs, contests, or acts on it now?
- [ ] **Payoff:** Does this chapter deliver something now, not merely promise later value?
- [ ] **Progression:** Are obstacles changing rather than repeating at a louder scale?
- [ ] **Language:** When read aloud, do viewpoint, rhythm, dialogue, imagery, and humor produce the intended effect?
- [ ] **Edition:** If translated, does it work as native target-language fiction while preserving canon and ambiguity?

## The governing rule

> **Every unit earns the next.**

A sentence earns the next sentence. A beat earns the next beat. A scene earns the next scene. It does so by adding at least one of these:

- **pressure:** a desire, deadline, danger, obstacle, obligation, or cost;
- **uncertainty:** a specific question the reader wants answered;
- **change:** a choice, discovery, reversal, consequence, or relationship shift;
- **meaning:** a concrete detail that makes the event matter to this character;
- **pleasure:** humor, beauty, voice, recognition, awe, dread, or satisfying competence.

If several consecutive units only repeat, explain, or confirm what the reader already understands, compress or replace them.

## Before drafting: the story contract

Write one short answer for each.

- **Intended experience:** What should reading this story feel like?
- **Human center:** Whose desire, relationship, question, or contradiction carries it?
- **Story engine:** What repeatedly generates new situations rather than the same situation again?
- **Form:** What shape fits—arc, spiral, mystery, journey, accumulation, episodic chain, network, or another pattern?
- **Promises:** What must this genre and premise deliver on the page?
- **Voice:** What is distinctive about the attention, humor, rhythm, imagery, and distance of the telling?
- **Boundaries:** Which shortcuts, clichés, power violations, or moral evasions would betray the project?

Put genre-, culture-, audience-, and language-specific answers in a separate overlay using [`story-craft-overlay-template.md`](story-craft-overlay-template.md). Do not mistake one successful story’s overlay for universal craft.

## Before writing a scene or chapter

Answer these seven questions. If the scene is intentionally contemplative or associative, translate “want” and “resistance” into the relevant emotional or formal movement rather than forcing an action plot.

1. **Who wants what now?**
2. **Why must this happen now rather than later?**
3. **What pressure, resistance, or uncertainty keeps the outcome open?**
4. **What does the viewpoint character do—not merely witness or learn?**
5. **What choice, revelation, or turn changes the available future?**
6. **What does it cost or alter materially, emotionally, or relationally?**
7. **What does the reader receive here, before being asked to continue?**

Write the scene delta in one sentence:

> Because **event** happened and **character** chose **action**, **new state** is now true.

If there is no meaningful delta, summarize, combine, relocate, or cut.

## While drafting: the page-value test

At each natural beat, ask:

- Is there a live question, desire, or expectation?
- Did this beat add pressure, knowledge, choice, consequence, relationship movement, or pleasure?
- Is the new information changing behavior now, or is it detached explanation?
- Is the character causing the scene, resisting it, or paying for it?
- Is the meaning embodied in action, object, sensation, or behavior before it is explained?
- Is this the first and strongest version of the point, or a repetition?

A quiet passage may pass through voice, intimacy, image, thought, or pattern. A loud passage may fail because nothing meaningful changes.

## Character standard

For every principal character, verify:

- a present desire or obligation;
- a value, fear, wound, loyalty, or self-story shaping how they pursue it;
- agency sufficient to alter another person’s options;
- a contradiction or capacity to surprise that remains psychologically credible;
- a relationship in which both parties can demand, deny, misunderstand, or change something real;
- consequences that persist after the scene.

A supporting character is not complete if their only function is to admire, explain, heal, permit, obstruct, or supply the protagonist.

After a major event, ask what remains bodily, materially, relationally, or behaviorally true two scenes later. Immediate pain without later residue is often intensity without consequence.

## Scene and pacing standard

- Enter when the situation becomes unstable; leave after the decisive turn and necessary emotional fact.
- Expand choice, discovery, confrontation, irreversible emotion, and distinctive pleasure.
- Compress repeated labor, travel, logistics, and explanation unless the process itself is the pleasure.
- Vary the source and scale of pressure; escalation is not the same obstacle made louder.
- Let people and systems learn from earlier moves. Opposition that never adapts makes later success feel pre-solved.
- Let reveals answer an existing question **and** create a new implication.
- Give a chapter local satisfaction as well as forward pressure.
- After major events, show changed behavior or material reality; do not substitute a thematic summary.

## Prose and dialogue standard

- Prefer precise nouns and active, specific verbs over abstract explanation and prefabricated phrasing.
- Let sentence length, syntax, sound, and paragraph shape embody the scene’s movement.
- Vary rhythm by intention, not by quota. Short is not automatically fast; long is not automatically slow.
- Preserve image families without repeating the same metaphor mechanically.
- Keep viewpoint attention selective: describe what this consciousness would notice now.
- In dialogue, every speaker is doing something—testing, bargaining, deflecting, protecting, entertaining, concealing, or changing status.
- Remove dialogue that only repeats what both characters and the reader know.
- Read key pages aloud. Fix unintended monotony, breathlessness, drag, or false grandeur.

## Revision order

Do not polish sentences that may need to be cut.

### Pass 0 — Intent and strengths

- Restate the story contract.
- List the lines, scenes, relationships, jokes, images, and surprises that must be protected.
- Record actual reader reactions, including where they laughed, skimmed, stopped, predicted, or cared.

### Pass 1 — Reverse outline

For every scene, record:

| Scene | Want/question | Pressure | Choice/turn | Delta | Reader payoff | Next pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Cut or combine scenes with duplicate jobs. Repair missing causality before adding prose.

### Pass 2 — Attention and significance

- Mark where each live question begins, progresses, and resolves.
- Mark stretches that offer no new pressure, uncertainty, change, meaning, or pleasure.
- Replace repeated thesis statements with consequence, relationship, or discovery.
- Ask “Why should the reader care **now**, in this body and this relationship?”
- Flag rescues, confessions, evidence, and perfectly timed arrivals that succeed only through invisible authorial help.
- If a quiet or cumulative unit has no local delta, name the larger pattern it advances and where the reader can perceive that development.

### Pass 3 — Character and emotion

- Track who makes each consequential choice.
- Give passive or functional characters leverage.
- Verify that emotion changes action rather than appearing only as labels or reflection.
- Make victories and losses alter later behavior.
- Check agency distribution: who chooses, benefits, pays, is overruled, and can withdraw consent?
- Give the thematic countervalue a credible benefit or painful truth; do not defeat it only through eloquent dialogue.

### Pass 4 — Scene and information

- Move exposition to the moment of need, disagreement, payment, or decision.
- Start later and end earlier where possible.
- Expand under-dramatized turns; summarize stable process.
- Check viewpoint, suspense, reveal order, and chapter payoff.

### Pass 5 — Language

- Read aloud for movement and music.
- Replace vague abstraction, stale imagery, weak verb phrases, and accidental repetition.
- Vary syntax and paragraph rhythm according to effect.
- Protect deliberate simplicity, silence, oddness, dialect, and culturally specific form.

### Pass 6 — Continuity, copyedit, proof

- Verify time, distance, injuries, objects, names, information access, power limits, terminology, and cause/effect.
- Copyedit grammar, punctuation, typography, headings, and formatting.
- Proof the final rendered website and ebook, not only the source Markdown.

## Translation and localization standard

Treat each translation as a new literary edition.

1. **Lock canon:** preserve events, facts, relationships, custody, limitations, and deliberate ambiguity.
2. **Name the effect:** for each scene, record its intended pace, humor, tension, intimacy, status, and emotional turn.
3. **Rebuild in the target language:** preserve the effect, not sentence count, word order, omitted subjects, or paragraph count.
4. **Localize dialogue and rhythm:** use natural target-language tactics while preserving hierarchy and character intention.
5. **Read continuously:** harmonize voice across chapters and translators.
6. **Compare back to canon:** recheck numbers, objects, injuries, names, reveals, and power rules.

For Korean-to-English prose specifically:

- do not copy clipped Korean syntax mechanically;
- allow English sentences to connect cause, qualification, and perception where natural;
- keep short lines where they land a blow, joke, image, or decision—not as the default beat;
- recreate dry humor through timing and character attitude rather than literal structure;
- remove faux-archaic diction, defensive explanation, and repetitive subject starts;
- judge the English aloud as English fiction.

## Release gate

A draft is ready for independent QA when the answer to each is yes:

- Does the intended experience remain alive beyond the opening?
- Does every scene have a meaningful delta?
- Do principal characters alter outcomes through choices?
- Does each chapter provide present-tense value, not only setup?
- Are stakes felt through specific consequence and relationship?
- Is exposition attached to pressure or decision?
- Does the prose carry the reader sentence to sentence when read aloud?
- Have repetitions of premise, theme, and mechanism been cut?
- Are genre promises paid fairly?
- Are continuity and power constraints intact?
- For translations, does the target edition work as native prose while preserving canon?
- Has an independent reader checked where attention drops?

A release pass means no blocking failure remains. It does not mean the work is beyond improvement.

Classify findings honestly:

- **Blocker:** breaks the story contract, causality, agency, reader orientation, promised payoff, canon, or intended language experience.
- **Revision opportunity:** could sharpen pleasure or meaning but does not prevent the work from functioning.

Do not turn optional polish into endless delay, and do not relabel a broken reader experience as optional because the outline is technically complete.
