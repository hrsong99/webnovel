# Fiction craft fundamentals — research synthesis

This note records the reasoning behind the operational standard in `docs/fiction-craft-standard.md`. It is meant to outlast one series, genre, language, or publishing format.

## What this standard can and cannot do

No checklist guarantees great fiction. Craft choices are culturally situated, and teaching one dominant dramatic arc as universal erases other traditions and reader expectations.[2][17] Jane Alison likewise shows that narrative momentum can come from patterns such as spirals, meanders, cells, and networks rather than only tension rising to a single climax.[15]

The useful goal is therefore narrower: prevent avoidable failures, make the intended reader experience explicit, and force each draft to justify its choices. A quiet literary scene, comic romance chapter, mystery reveal, and progression-fantasy battle may use different forms while still being accountable for attention, change, character agency, and language.

## 1. Attention is renewed, not won once

A strong opening is only a loan. Each subsequent unit must renew attention by changing what the reader wants to know, fear, hope for, understand, or enjoy.

Research on curiosity describes a drive to close salient information gaps, while also warning that people avoid information they do not want to think about.[8] Brewer and Lichtenstein distinguish suspense (outcome delayed), curiosity (causes delayed), and surprise (a model revised by withheld information), which helps avoid treating every hook as the same device.[31] Recent work on narrative suspense frames suspense around questions whose answers could terminate the inquiry.[7] For fiction, the practical implication is not “hide everything.” It is to make a specific live question matter, offer partial progress, and replace answered questions with stronger ones.

Ursula K. Le Guin gives the sentence-level equivalent: the chief duty of a narrative sentence is to lead to the next sentence; forward movement is inseparable from rhythm.[6] This is broader than plot speed. Humor, beauty, dread, recognition, and emotional intimacy can all pull a reader forward.

**Operational consequence:** mark long stretches that offer no new pressure, understanding, relationship shift, surprise, or verbal pleasure. A passage that only confirms an already-understood premise is a likely drag even when its information is relevant.

## 2. Change is the basic unit

Scene-and-structure traditions emphasize cause and effect and ask scenes to move the struggle forward.[13] That is a useful diagnostic, not a command that every scene end in literal disaster. A scene earns its length when its end state differs meaningfully from its beginning: a decision closes an option, information changes a plan, a relationship acquires a debt, a cost becomes real, or a value is tested.

External event alone is insufficient. Scene-blueprint approaches are most useful when they connect action to internal logic and urgency rather than treating plotting as a sequence of incidents.[14]

**Operational consequence:** every scene receives a one-line delta: “Because this happened and this person chose X, now Y is true.” If that sentence cannot be written, compress, combine, relocate, or cut the scene.

## 3. Character is pressure revealed through choice

Forster’s distinction between flat and round characters remains useful when treated as a spectrum and a role-dependent choice, not a moral ranking: some characters can be legible types, while central characters need credible capacity to surprise.[4] Gardner and Burroway’s broad craft frameworks both place characterization alongside plot, point of view, scene, and language rather than beneath them.[5][12]

A practical character model needs four things:

1. a present desire or obligation;
2. a value, fear, wound, or self-conception that shapes the method;
3. enough agency to alter outcomes;
4. choices that reveal contradiction and create consequence.

“Desire” here is deliberately broad. Aristotle links character to choice, while Robert Olen Butler’s language of yearning allows diffuse, contradictory, or partly unconscious orientation.[18][19] A character may seek, protect, avoid, endure, investigate, or refuse; agency includes speech, withholding, care, witness, and constrained action, not only domination or victory.

Supporting characters fail when they function only as information, permission, healing, admiration, or opposition. They become alive when their interests are not fully compatible with the protagonist’s and when they can impose a real cost.

## 4. Stakes must become felt significance

Large death counts, world-ending threats, and declarations of injustice do not automatically feel important. Gardner’s practical formulation connects reader care to what has visible bearing on someone or something the reader has learned to value.[12] Significance comes from specificity and consequence: who loses what, what future becomes impossible, what relationship changes, what compromise stains the victory, and what ordinary object now means something different.

This is especially important in political or institutional fiction. Salesses warns that a story of resistance can provide satisfying catharsis without producing genuine engagement; repeatedly representing the same system may become a resting place rather than a new encounter.[16] Fiction need not incite action, but it should not mistake repeated explanation of its moral position for dramatic development.

**Operational consequence:** after an important event, dramatize a changed behavior, relationship, material condition, or choice before explaining its theme. If the scene already made the meaning legible, trust it.

Emotional consequence should also persist. Classical rasa theory is a useful corrective to purely private or therapeutic models of emotion: represented conditions and expressions are arranged into an aesthetic experience for the audience.[22] The practical test is whether a wound, intimacy, victory, or betrayal changes later attention, language, body, choice, or relationship—not whether the character delivers an explicit feeling statement.

## 5. Information arrives under pressure

Worldbuilding and backstory are strongest when someone needs the information now, disputes it, pays for it, or changes course because of it. Explanation without an immediate decision often stalls the story even when the underlying idea is excellent.

Use scene for unstable moments involving choice, conflict, discovery, or irreversible emotion. Use summary for travel, repetition, logistics, or stable processes unless the process itself contains the story’s pleasure. Genette’s distinctions among scene, summary, pause, and ellipsis are useful here because pacing is an allocation of discourse time, not a synonym for action or short sentences.[32] Expansion and compression are controls, not markers of literary seriousness.

A reveal should do at least two jobs: answer something already alive and create a new implication, obligation, danger, or desire.

## 6. Form is selected for the intended experience

Salesses argues that craft language and workshop expectations contain cultural values; Alison demonstrates viable patterns beyond the conventional arc.[2][15] Phelan’s rhetorical model further treats narrative as someone telling a particular story to a particular audience, in a particular situation, for particular purposes.[33] Therefore the standard must ask what experience the work intends before judging its structure.

Genre and format overlays may add constraints:

- mystery emphasizes fair questions, clue control, and reinterpretation;
- romance emphasizes relational movement and emotional payoff;
- horror emphasizes vulnerability, anticipation, and violation;
- progression fantasy emphasizes legible limits, earned competence, and fair payoff;
- comedy emphasizes setup, expectation, reversal, timing, and character truth;
- serial fiction emphasizes local satisfaction plus renewed forward pressure;
- literary or nonlinear fiction may advance by image, pattern, association, consciousness, or accumulating implication.

The overlay may change the shape of movement, but not the need for an intended effect and evidence that the prose is producing it.

This openness is not permission to extract living traditions as interchangeable templates.

Le Guin’s carrier-bag essay challenges weapon-centered heroic form.[21]

Morrison exposes how apparently neutral literary ideas can depend on racialized absences.[20]

Finnegan shows that oral literature depends on performance, participants, medium, and occasion.[23]

Archibald’s Indigenous storywork joins story with culturally specific responsibilities and relationships.[24]

Use such work to question defaults, not to label a borrowed surface structure “universal” or “non-Western.”

## 7. Prose has meaning, movement, and music

Le Guin treats sound and rhythm as fundamental physical elements of prose, not decoration; dullness, choppiness, flow, pace, and strength are heard as well as understood.[1][6] Sentence variety should follow attention and action rather than a quota. Short sentences can strike, accelerate, or deaden. Long sentences can immerse, accumulate, delay, or blur.

Orwell’s critique of stale imagery, vagueness, prefabricated phrases, abstract nouns, and weak verb phrases is a valuable clarity pass, but even Orwell says to break a rule rather than write something barbarous.[3] Literary prose also needs play, texture, surprise, and voice.

**Operational consequence:** read important pages aloud. Revise where syntax fights the intended emotion, where every sentence has the same beat, where abstraction replaces an image or action, or where a clever line calls attention away from the scene.

## 8. Dialogue is action

A dialogue turn should do more than transfer facts. Someone is trying to persuade, deflect, wound, test, protect, bargain, conceal, entertain, or change status. Distinct voices arise from different attention, history, power, vocabulary, and tactics—not merely catchphrases.

Good dialogue often lets literal content and social action differ. Exposition becomes more alive when the listener can resist, misunderstand, exploit, or impose a price on the information.

## 9. Revision proceeds from purpose to punctuation

Revising sentences before fixing scene purpose wastes effort and makes necessary cuts harder. The reliable order is:

1. name the intended reader experience and protect the strongest lines/scenes;
2. reverse-outline what actually happens and changes;
3. repair causality, pacing, agency, stakes, and structure;
4. repair viewpoint, information flow, and scene boundaries;
5. revise language, rhythm, imagery, and dialogue;
6. copyedit continuity, grammar, terminology, and formatting;
7. proof the final rendered form.

This macro-to-micro sequence also matches professional editorial practice: structural editing precedes stylistic and copy editing, while proofreading checks the publication-ready proof rather than reopening the book’s architecture.[25] Fiction continuity sheets should preserve names, chronology, injuries, knowledge, objects, world rules, and style decisions across those passes.[28]

Le Guin’s exercises make revision concrete through sound, sentence construction, point of view, and disciplined practice.[1][6]

Mantel describes analytical reading, repeated attempts to pin down the precise sentence, and the value of a listening ear who genuinely wants to know what happens next.[10]

Burroway’s long-running guide similarly treats revision and theme as part of narrative craft rather than mere typo cleanup.[5]

## 10. Translation is effect reconstruction

A literary translation is not successful because it preserves source sentence length or word order. It must preserve event, fact, relationship, ambiguity, image systems, and character intention while rebuilding rhythm, emphasis, humor, hierarchy, and readability in the target language.

Korean can create speed through omitted subjects, compact connective logic, and short paragraphing. Copying that surface pattern into English can produce monotony, false portent, or a sequence of defensive subject-verb sentences. The English edition should therefore preserve the Korean scene’s function and personality while allowing more syntactic connection, paragraph shaping, idiomatic dialogue, and rhythmic variation.

This is not merely stylistic preference. Korean literary prose can use implicit subjects and grammatically carried ambiguity that English must redistribute rather than erase or duplicate mechanically.[26][27] Translation review therefore needs three separate checks: event fidelity, pragmatic fidelity (knowledge, hierarchy, implication, intended response), and aesthetic fidelity (pace, humor, distance, surprise, and emphasis).

Korean translator Jack Jung describes translation as carrying something through the ruin of one home toward an uncertain future—a useful reminder that the result is a new literary object, not a transparent duplicate.[9] The translation still needs independent continuous editing because local sentence fidelity can coexist with a globally inconsistent voice.

| Onea et al. and Golman–Loewenstein | Specific narrative questions and information gaps | Curiosity is one attention engine, not a complete theory of fiction.[7][8] |

## 11. Evidence beats formula

Do not assign a fake literary score. Use observable evidence:

- where a reader’s live question changes;
- where a character makes a consequential choice;
- what is newly true after a scene;
- where the reader receives payoff or pleasure;
- where prose is read aloud without unintended drag;
- where independent readers stop, skim, laugh, predict, misunderstand, or care.

A checklist is a conversation with the draft. It is not an algorithm for producing art.
